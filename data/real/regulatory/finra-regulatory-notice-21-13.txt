WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
  sim/portfolio.py::Account._count_day_trade counts intraday round trips. The
  rule text alone ("the purchasing and selling ... of the same security on the
  same day ... except for positions held overnight") does not determine the
  arithmetic when a participant trades the same symbol five times in a session.
  This Notice is the only place FINRA works example sequences out by hand, so it
  is the specification the venue reproduces. The six answers below are quoted
  verbatim and are asserted one-by-one in
  tests/test_portfolio.py::TestFinraDayTradeExamples.

RULE TEXT, quoted
  "FINRA Rule 4210(f)(8)(B)(i) defines 'day trading' to mean the purchasing and
  selling or the selling and purchasing of the same security on the same day in
  a margin account except for positions held overnight FINRA Rule
  4210(f)(8)(B)(ii) defines a 'pattern day trader' as a customer who executes
  four or more day trades within five business days."

  Interpretation /02, quoted:
  "Rather than counting day trades based on the number of transactions a
  customer executes to establish or increase a position that is liquidated on
  the same day (as set out in Interpretation /01 above) a firm may instead count
  day trades based on the number of times during the day that the day trading
  customer changes its trading direction (i.e., changes from buying a particular
  security to selling it, or changes from selling a particular security to
  buying it)."

THE SIX WORKED EXAMPLES, quoted verbatim with the Notice's own answers
  Example A:  09:30 Buy 250 ABC / 09:31 Buy 250 ABC / 13:00 Sell 500 ABC
              "The customer has executed one day trade."
  Example B:  09:30 Buy 100 ABC / 09:31 Sell 100 ABC / 09:32 Buy 100 ABC /
              13:00 Sell 100 ABC
              "The customer has executed two day trades."
  Example C:  09:30 Buy 500 ABC / 13:00 Sell 100 ABC / 13:01 Sell 100 ABC /
              13:03 Sell 300 ABC
              "The customer has executed one day trade."
  Example D:  09:30 Buy 250 ABC / 09:31 Buy 300 ABC / 13:01 Buy 100 ABC /
              13:02 Sell 150 ABC / 13:03 Sell 175 ABC
              "The customer has executed one day trade."
  Example E:  09:30 Buy 199 ABC / 09:31 Buy 142 ABC / 13:00 Sell 1 ABC /
              13:01 Buy 45 ABC / 13:02 Sell 100 ABC / 13:03 Sell 200 ABC
              "The customer has executed two day trades."
  Example F:  09:30 Buy 200 ABC / 09:30 Buy 100 XYZ / 13:00 Sell 100 ABC /
              13:00 Sell 100 XYZ
              "The customer has executed two day trades"

WHAT THAT RULES OUT
  Examples C, D and E rule out the two implementations that a reader of the
  rule text alone would write: counting closing fills (C would be three) and
  counting every fill that reduces the day's quantity (D would be two).
  Consecutive same-direction fills must be merged into one clip and the count
  taken over clip transitions. The venue therefore counts a day trade when an
  accumulating clip is followed by a reducing clip that gives back shares the
  account opened in that session.

ENDNOTE, quoted
  "A customer is not considered a 'pattern day trader' if the number of day
  trades is 6 percent or less of the total trades for a five business day
  period."
