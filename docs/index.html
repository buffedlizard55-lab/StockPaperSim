Season 2 overview · Leaderboard · Verified trade ledger · MasterFeed register · Data custody

Twenty strategies. One real market year. Every trade auditable.

StockPaperSim runs a one-year paper-trading stock competition in the style of TradingView's The Leap, Trade Ideas' PM Challenge and the CandleCharts Showdown: each participant gets 100,000 USD of paper capital, a unique username, and a written strategy. They are ranked on total return. What makes this one different is that the market they trade is a replay of the real S&P 500 and VIX path, their orders go through a quoted-spread, depth, impact and regulatory-fee model, and every order, fill, rejection, mark and margin event is written to memory for later analysis.

None of these participants manages risk. That was the design brief: each strategy is built to maximise return only. The consequence is on full display below - the winner is up +108% with a 44% drawdown, and the last place participant lost 92% of the account.

Simulating real trades on US equities (Nasdaq, NYSE, S&P 500)

Can strategies place upcoming trades and simulate a real trading experience? Yes. The venue models real US equity market mechanics: Level 2 order book depth, quoted spread crossing under Reg NMS Rule 612, Almgren-Chriss square-root market impact, dated SEC §31 ($20.60/M) and FINRA TAF ($0.000195/sh) fees, and Reg T 50% initial margin. Strategies and users can stage upcoming orders for the next session opening bell (09:30 ET) or closing bell (16:00 ET), track real-time slippage, test liquidity constraints, and commit fills to a verified SHA-256 ledger.

The three questions this site answers

  1. Who won, and by how much? The leaderboard ranks all 20 participants on total return, with drawdown, Sharpe, beta, trade count, win rate, profit factor, execution cost and turnover beside each one.
  2. Why did each strategy make or lose money? Every participant page carries a generated post-mortem: the return decomposed into beta and residual, the style premia that were actually live in this window, the symbols that paid and the symbols that hurt, the cost of execution, and an explicit answer to "did it work, and why?".
  3. How much of the result is luck? One calendar year is one draw. The robustness panel re-runs the same competition five more times on the same real index path with different idiosyncratic draws, and reports mean, median, best and worst per participant.

Honesty about the data

This project was built under a hard constraint: no invented numbers. Every figure is either a real published observation, a simulation explicitly labelled as one, or a computation over the two. The data provenance page lists, field by field, which is which; the source register lists all 73 external references with the verification status of each, including 31 academic citations that could not be retrieved inside the build environment and are therefore marked KNOWN-NOT-FETCHED for manual review.

Anything unusual found along the way - a VIX print on a market holiday, a regulatory fee tier that is adopted but not yet operative, a calibration choice that trades one kind of accuracy for another - is written down in Irregularities rather than quietly resolved. What the simulation cannot do at all is in Limitations.

StockPaperSim is a research and education project. Every price series a participant traded on is either a real published observation (FRED S&P 500 and VIX closes, Yahoo Finance monthly SPY bars and AAPL bars/dividends) or an explicitly labelled simulation of it. Nothing here is investment advice, and no result on this site should be read as evidence about a strategy's real future performance. See Limitations.
