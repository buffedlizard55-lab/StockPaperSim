Irregularities

Everything found during the build that did not behave the way a naive reading of the sources would predict - data quirks, regulation in transition, sources that could not be verified, and model choices where two defensible answers disagreed. Each one is recorded rather than quietly resolved, because a simulation that hides its own problems cannot be audited.

IR-01lowVIXCLS has observations on dates when SP500 does notThe two real FRED series used as market factors do not cover exactly the same dates. Over the Season 1 window, VIXCLS carries values on 7 dates where SP500 is blank. SP500 blanks are market closures (all ten cross-check against the published Nasdaq holiday schedule); VIX closes are nevertheless published on some of those dates because the VIX is computed from SPX option prices, and CBOE option settlement/holiday conventions do not line up one-for-one with the cash-index publication calendar. Taking the two series at face value would have produced a calendar with sessions the cash market never opened.
  • https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SP500
  • https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/VIXCLS
  • https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/stock-market-holiday-schedule

Resolution: The trading calendar is built from the SP500 series only. VIX observations on non-sessions are dropped and counted; the count is reported in the run manifest as vix_observations_dropped_on_closed_dates. Every session carries a real VIX value.

IR-04lowThe 2024 Reg NMS half-penny tick tier is codified but exempt - verified, not assumedThe SEC's September 2024 Reg NMS amendments (Release 34-101070, published at 89 FR 81774) add a $0.005 minimum pricing increment for NMS stocks whose time-weighted average quoted spread is at or below $0.015, and cut the Rule 610(c) access-fee cap from $0.003 to $0.001 per share. That amended text IS in the current Code of Federal Regulations: 17 CFR 242.612(b)(2), retrieved from the eCFR on 2026-09-17 (title 17 up to date as of 2026-09-15), reads $0.01 where the TWAQS exceeded $0.015 and $0.005 where it did not. It is nevertheless NOT in force. The Commission granted a partial stay on 12-Dec-2024 (Release 34-101899) pending judicial review; the D.C. Circuit denied the petitions for review in October 2025; the Commission then granted temporary exemptive relief to the first business day of November 2026 on 31-Oct-2025; and Release 34-105656 (11-Jun-2026, published 15-Jun-2026 as document 2026-11997) extended that relief for Rules 600(b)(89)(i)(F), 610(c) and 612 until the first business day of November 2027. The entire Season 1 window (2025-09-17 to 2026-09-16) falls inside the exemption period. This entry was originally written as 'genuinely uncertain'; it is now resolved from primary sources and downgraded from medium to low.

IR-06mediumThe intraday-range calibration that best fits real SPY data contradicts the documented overnight/intraday variance splitA grid search over the wick coefficient and the share of daily variance placed in the overnight gap, scored against 13 real Yahoo SPY monthly bars, preferred wick=0.25 with overnight share=0.05 (mean absolute difference 0.401 percentage points). That overnight share is well below what the published literature on overnight versus intraday return variance supports, so the best-fitting point buys range accuracy by misrepresenting where variance occurs - and the overnight leg matters directly to one participant (@OvernightCarry_NO) and to every opening-gap decision.

Resolution: Compromise committed: overnight share 0.15, wick coefficient 0.20, mean absolute difference 0.522pp against the real monthly ranges. Both constants carry this note in marketdata.py, and the audit table is published on the data-provenance page so the cost of the compromise is visible.

IR-07highEvent-driven strategies run on price/volume proxies, not on real event dataTwo participants are built around information events: @DriftRider_PEAD (post-earnings-announcement drift) and @SqueezeHunter_TF (short-squeeze / low-float momentum). Neither real earnings dates and EPS surprises nor short interest, days-to-cover or float data was obtainable offline, so both use a proxy: a large multi-session price move on elevated volume. A proxy of that kind conflates genuine information events with ordinary volatility, and the two have opposite continuation behaviour.
  • https://doi.org/10.2307/2490899

Resolution: Both strategy specs declare this as a known failure mode in the first line, the narrative surfaces it as a standing caveat on every run, and the site repeats it on the participant pages. Their results should be read as 'what a price/volume event filter did', never as 'what post-earnings drift earns'.

IR-08highThe overnight-premium participant is filled after the gap it is trying to capture@OvernightCarry_NO's edge is the overnight return: buy at the close, sell at the next open. Its measured performance is therefore structurally biased against the strategy - or, on days when the gap reverses, in favour of it - and neither direction reflects the real overnight premium.

Resolution: Flagged in the strategy spec, in the narrative caveats and on the participant page. Fixing it properly needs a close-auction execution path (an order type that fills at the session close print), which is listed as remaining work. | UPDATED 2026-09-17: superseded by IR-34. The venue grew an end-of-session execution ticket (Order.at_close), so the overnight strategy's entry is now worked on the final interval and the structural understatement is closed rather than merely disclosed. The season was re-run; @OvernightCarry_NO's published number changed because it is now a different trade from the one Season 1 measured before this pass, and the residual limitation (the closing print is a synthetic interval, not a tape print) stays in its spec.

IR-09lowEleven dividend schedules are declared approximations; only AAPL's are realAAPL carries four real ex-dates and amounts captured from the Yahoo chart API. The other dividend payers in the universe (SPY, QQQ, IWM, MSFT, GOOGL, META, NVDA, JPM, XOM, UNH, CAT, WMT) needed schedules too - without them the buy-and-hold control would be understated by roughly one dividend yield per year and that bias would leak into every relative comparison.

Resolution: Declared yields with quarterly ex-month patterns approximating each issuer's real cycle are attached and labelled 'scenario-declared' in the instrument provenance. To avoid double counting, the replay generator subtracts the declared window dividend yield from the price drift for non-anchored names, so declared total return equals price path plus cash dividends. Ex-dates falling on a non-session are paid on the next session and raise IR-10.

IR-14mediumRuntime: orders rejected by the broker-side pre-trade margin checkAn order that would breach the gross leverage cap and could not be resized (not even one share fits) is rejected, with the measured leverage in the reason. In the primary scenario @SpreadHarvester_MM had 131 such rejections: its inventory drifted the book above 2.0x gross through price movement, after which the broker only accepted risk-reducing orders.

Resolution: Aggregated per participant and per normalised reason so a single stuck book does not produce hundreds of near-identical flags. Risk-reducing orders are never blocked, so a participant can always trade its way back inside the cap.

Resolution: 195 clips for @SpreadHarvester_MM and 44 for @KitchenSink_AllIn in the primary scenario; both are reported on the participant pages.

IR-16highFactor premia measured on this replay are artefacts of declared parametersThe narrative engine attributes performance partly by comparing each strategy against realised long/short factor premia (momentum, reversal, beta, low-vol, liquidity) computed from the same replay. Those premia are real numbers about this competition, but the cross-sectional drifts that generate them are declared scenario inputs in sim/universe.py, not observed market data. Resolution: Every participant narrative carries the caveat automatically, and the market page repeats it in a callout above the factor panel. The real long-horizon evidence for each premium is cited separately, with its verification status, on the strategy pages.

IR-17lowThe SEC Section 31 fee rate was zero for the first two-thirds of the competition windowThe FY2026 annual adjustment order sets the Section 31 transaction fee rate at $0.00 per $1,000,000 from 2025-09-01 and $20.60 per $1,000,000 from 2026-04-04. A zero rate is unusual and is a consequence of the timing of appropriations and the Commission's fee-setting process, not of a policy decision to abolish the fee. A model that used a single constant rate across the window would misprice sells by the full amount for seven months.
  • https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026-03-04/2026-04233/order-making-fiscal-year-2026-annual-adjustments-to-transaction-fee-rates
  • https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/MicroNews.aspx?id=OTA2026-14

Resolution: The cost model is date-dependent: rate_for() picks the rate in force on the trade date. Sell-side regulatory cost is therefore exactly zero before 2026-04-04 and $20.60 per million after it, matching the order.

IR-19mediumSingle-path cross-sectional dispersion is driven by idiosyncratic draws, not by a volatility-model errorIn the primary scenario the standard deviation of one-year returns across the 17 instruments is 47.3 percentage points, with NVDA +147%, JPM +60%, XOM -43% and UNH -52%. That is wide. Diagnosing it showed realised total volatility matching the predicted volatility closely for every name (NVDA 50.3% predicted vs 49.7% realised; JPM 22.5% vs 21.9%), so the vol model is correct and the spread is the expected consequence of cumulative idiosyncratic draws over one year (XOM z = -2.68, JPM z = +2.17, NVDA z = +1.63).

Resolution: The declared idiosyncratic volatilities were deliberately NOT shrunk to make one path look tidier - that would bias every result toward the mean. Instead the competition is run over six scenarios and the robustness panel reports mean, median, best and worst per participant, which is the honest way to show how much of a single-season rank is the draw.

IR-20highFound in review: a market-making participant could size quotes in the billions@SpreadHarvester_MM sized each resting quote at 10% of 21-day ADV without applying the inventory cap its own spec declares. On SPY that is roughly 6 million shares, about $3.9bn of notional, on a $100,000 account - and because the strategy constructed Order objects directly it bypassed the margin screen that Context.orders_to_targets applies. The account reached $140,652,977 of equity in six sessions.

Resolution: Two fixes, both committed. (1) The strategy now sizes min(10% of ADV, 60% of equity / price), matching its spec. (2) Defence in depth: the engine runs a broker-side pre-trade risk check on EVERY order regardless of how it was built, resizing to the largest permissible quantity or rejecting, and the event is raised as IR-14/IR-15. IR-21mediumFound in review: VWAP-slicing every market order handed participants free intraday driftMarket orders were spread across the whole session by volume weight, which is what a VWAP algorithm does, not what a market order does. Because the decision price is the opening mid, the difference between the session average price and the open was booked as favourable 'drift'. For @MomentumMax_12x1 that was -$1,857 on $614k of paper notional (about -30bp of phantom benefit), and it varied by strategy with how much each one traded - a systematic bias in the ranking, not a cost.
  • https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v44.5.28

Resolution: Slicing is now capacity-driven: an order trades at the touch and only spans further intervals when its size exceeds what one interval can absorb inside the participation limit. The implementation-shortfall decomposition now separates friction (spread, depth, impact, fees) from timing (drift) so neither can hide inside the other.

IR-22mediumFound in review: round-trip reconstruction dropped the flipped leg of a position reversalWhen a fill took a position through zero (long 100 becomes short 200 on a 300-share sell), the trade ledger closed the original lot and discarded the residual 200 shares, so the entire P&L of the new position vanished from the attribution. For @DriftRider_PEAD the unexplained residual in its P&L decomposition was +$20,169.

Resolution: The reconstruction now opens a new lot in the opposite direction for the residual at the same fill price. IR-23mediumFound in review: beta was regressed one session out of alignmentThe equity curve holds one mark per session, so its daily-return series starts one session later than the market-return series it was paired with. The regression therefore compared each participant's return against the previous day's index move, which produced near-zero betas for obviously levered books (@BetaChaser_3xProxy measured beta 0.07 while carrying 2x gross in high-beta names) and inflated alphas to nonsense (+104.7% per year).

Resolution: Both series are now built over identical session ranges and length-checked before the regression. The same participant measures beta 3.92 with alpha +44.9%/yr, and the market-neutral participant (@PairsArb_ZScore2) measures beta -0.01, which is the sanity check that the alignment is right.

IR-24lowQuoted spreads from a pure volatility-scaled model were two orders of magnitude too wideAn early version derived the quoted spread from volatility (spread = 0.20 x daily sigma x price), which produced $0.51 on NVDA - 27 basis points. NVDA's real quoted spread is one cent, about 0.5bp. The error came from ignoring that liquid US equities are tick-constrained: competition compresses the touch to the minimum increment whenever the tick is small relative to the price, which is exactly why the SEC's 2024 amendments introduced a finer tick for the tightest names.
  • https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-242/section-242.612
  • https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-242/section-242.605

Resolution: The spread model is now tick-based: a whole number of Rule 612 increments, scaled by sqrt(sigma x sqrt(VIX factor) / (tick/price)) and clamped by per-liquidity-tier tick bounds plus an absolute basis-point cap. At the season open this gives 1 tick on SPY, NVDA, AAPL, JPM and RIVN and 2 ticks on TSLA and CVNA; all-in execution cost lands at 0.5bp for a $100k order in NVDA and about 10bp for 50,000 shares of RIVN, consistent with published execution-quality statistics.

IR-25mediumThe Commission has proposed rescinding the Rule 611 order protection rule, which this simulation assumesThe simulated order book never crosses and never trades through a better displayed price, because Rule 611 of Regulation NMS (17 CFR 242.611) makes the best protected quotation a trade-through protection point. On 11-Jun-2026, alongside the exemptive order that pushed the tick-size and access-fee amendments to November 2027, the Commission proposed rescinding the Order Protection Rule; Chairman Atkins' statement of the same date records that proposal and directs staff to review Rules 610(c) and 612 by the end of 2026. If Rule 611 is rescinded, displayed quotations would no longer be protected, off-exchange and manual execution would no longer have to route to the NBBO, and the price-improvement and trade-through assumptions baked into sim/microstructure.py would describe a market that no longer exists. Nothing in Season 1 is affected - the rule was in force for every session of the window - but any future season started after a rescission would need the venue model re-derived.
  • https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-statement-minimum-pricing-increments-access-fee-caps-061126
  • https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-242/section-242.611

Resolution: Flagged rather than modelled: Season 1 ran entirely under Rule 611 as it stands, so no result changes. The proposal is tracked here so a maintainer re-running a later season checks the status of Rule 611 first. If it is rescinded, the affected code is the no-cross / trade-through invariant in microstructure.OrderBook.match and the protected-quote assumption in the liquidity model's price-improvement statistics; both are documented inline with their Rule 611 citation.

IR-26mediumThe impact model uses a square-root exponent; the empirical paper it cites measured 3/5sim/microstructure.py prices temporary market impact as coefficient x sigma_daily x sqrt(shares / ADV), the square-root law. That functional form comes from the optimal-execution framework of Almgren & Chriss (2001), Journal of Risk 3(2):21-40. The empirical paper most often cited alongside it - Almgren, Thum, Hauptmann & Li (2005), 'Direct estimation of equity market impact', Risk 18(7):58-62, fitted to roughly 700,000 Citigroup US equity orders - explicitly REJECTS the square-root model for temporary impact as a function of trade rate and reports a 3/5 power law instead. Using 0.5 therefore understates the concavity of impact: at 10% of ADV the square-root form gives 0.316 sigma-units where a 0.6 exponent gives 0.251, i.e. this model charges about 26% MORE impact than the measured law at that size, and the gap widens for smaller orders and narrows for larger ones. Neither the publisher page for the 2005 article (checked 2026-09-17: risk.net returns 'we can't seem to find the page you're looking for') nor the author-hosted working paper (courant.nyu.edu, redirects to cims.nyu.edu and returns HTTP 403) was retrievable from this environment, so the exponent finding is cited from the literature rather than read from the paper here.
  • https://doi.org/10.21314/JOR.2001.041
  • https://www.risk.net/cut-through/2455921/direct-estimation-of-equity-market-impact

Resolution: The square-root form is retained deliberately: it is the convention every execution desk and vendor model uses, it keeps ImpactConfig.coefficient comparable across instruments, and switching exponents would change every cost number in Season 1 without a verified calibration to replace it. The deviation is documented here and in the ImpactConfig docstring. Making the exponent a config field (default 0.5, with a 0.6 scenario for sensitivity) is listed in research/REMAINING_WORK.json.

IR-28mediumDisplayed quotes were being re-centred off the Rule 612 tick grid, so the venue published prices no exchange could displayExecutionEngine.quote_at() builds the dealer ladder on the minimum-increment grid, then re-centres it on the permanently-impacted mid so that quotes track the intraday path. That re-centring added an arbitrary fraction (mid minus the raw ladder mid) to every level, which pushed the displayed bid and ask off the grid - the recorded opening quote for SPY on 2025-09-17 was bid 655.4003 / ask 655.4103, a perfectly legal one-tick spread at two prices that 17 CFR 242.612(b) does not permit anyone to display. Across the primary run this affected 8,462 of the recorded quote levels and 5,902 of the bid/ask fields carried on fill rows. Executed fill prices were NOT affected: the fill engine rounds to the grid separately, and all 2,973 fills in the primary run are exact multiples of the operative increment. The existing unit test did not catch it because it called MarketMakerPool.quotes() directly, which has always been grid-clean; the defect lived one level up, in the re-centring, and no test crossed that boundary. Found while writing the audit-trail test that checks the prices actually WRITTEN to memory rather than the prices the venue code computes in isolation.
  • https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-242/section-242.612
  • https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024-10-08/2024-21867/regulation-nms-minimum-pricing-increments-access-fees-and-transparency-of-better-priced-orders

Resolution: Fixed in sim/microstructure.py: the re-centring shift is now snapped to a whole number of minimum increments (round((mid - raw_mid) / tick) * tick), so the ladder still tracks the impacted mid to within half a tick, the quoted spread stays at exactly n_ticks, and the book cannot cross because every level moves by the same amount. Two tests now hold the line: tests/test_microstructure.py::test_every_displayed_level_is_a_legal_rule_612_increment walks every symbol and three intraday intervals and asserts each displayed level, the quoted spread and the mid-tracking bound; tests/test_memory.py::test_every_recorded_price_is_a_legal_rule_612_increment asserts it on the published audit trail itself, over every fill row and every quote row in memory. A companion test, test_the_decision_price_is_deliberately_off_grid, pins the other half of the distinction: the price a strategy decided at stays the unrounded intraday mark, so the tick cost is charged rather than handed back. Season 1 was re-run and the site rebuilt after the fix, so the numbers published here are the post-fix numbers.

IR-29mediumLimit-order marketability is a knife edge: a sub-tick venue change moved one participant by 28.3pp and reshuffled 9 of 20 ranksMeasured, not estimated - and measured twice, because the first measurement was wrong. The IR-28 fix snapped displayed quotes back onto the Rule 612 grid: a change of at most half a tick, half a cent on every name in this universe. Re-running the identical seed and config afterwards moved @OneBigBet_Concentra by +28.3pp (20.6% to 48.9%) with closed trades rising from 36 to 55. The first version of this entry also reported the surrounding shifts as though they were all caused by the tick fix. They were not: that comparison was confounded by IR-30, the set-iteration bug, which meant neither side of the comparison was reproducible in the first place. The numbers below are the corrected measurement, taken with PYTHONHASHSEED pinned to 0 on both sides so that the tick snapping is the only difference between the two runs: @OverreactionFade_LT +32.88pp (40.58 to 73.46, trades 67 to 70); @OneBigBet_Concentra +28.30pp (20.63 to 48.94, trades 36 to 55); @MeanRev_Z2Sigma +9.37pp; @IlliquidRocket_Degen -7.35pp; @SqueezeHunter_TF -5.65pp (3.80 to -1.85, which flips its published verdict from 'made money but lagged the index' to 'lost money'); @DriftRider_PEAD +5.12pp; @KitchenSink_AllIn -4.33pp; @BetaChaser_3xProxy +0.08pp (the winner, 108.31 to 108.39). Ten of the twenty participants were unchanged to within 0.05pp; ten of twenty changed rank; mean absolute shift 4.67pp. The mechanism is discrete, not continuous: whether a resting limit order is marketable depends on which side of the touch it sits, so moving the touch by a fraction of a tick flips fills on and off, and because every strategy is path dependent - position, inventory, cash and margin available for the next order, subsequent signals - one flipped fill compounds through the rest of the year. Rising trade counts (36 to 55) are the visible symptom: these are not the same trades at slightly different prices, they are different trades.

Resolution: Recorded rather than smoothed over, because it is a property of the design and not a bug to patch - and because the first, confounded measurement is itself evidence of how easily a plausible number gets published. Four consequences are now carried explicitly. (1) The season's ranking is sensitive to sub-tick venue detail, so no single-path ranking should be read as a skill ordering; that is what the six-scenario robustness panel is for, and it is why the site publishes mean, median, best, worst and standard deviation per participant rather than one number. (2) Every measurement quoted in this register is now taken with PYTHONHASHSEED pinned, so it can be reproduced by anyone re-running the two commands. (3) tests/fixtures.py pins the published headline in one place with a change history, so an engine change shows up as one explicit diff instead of a scattering of failing assertions. (4) A sensitivity harness is registered as remaining work (P1): re-run under a small perturbation of each venue parameter - tick snapping, hidden liquidity, spread ticks, the impact exponent at 0.5 vs 0.6, maker rebate on and off - and publish the distribution of rank changes, so a reader can see which results are robust and which are knife-edge.

IR-30highThe published season was not reproducible: three strategies iterated a set, so PYTHONHASHSEED changed the leaderboardThe most serious defect found in this project, and the one the CI gate caught rather than a human. Three strategies - @DriftRider_PEAD, @SqueezeHunter_TF and @OverreactionFade_LT - built their held-position collection as a set comprehension (`held = {s for s in ctx.symbols if ctx.position(s)}`) and then looped over it to build the list of exits. CPython salts string hashing per process, so set iteration order changes from one interpreter to the next. Exit order determines the order sell orders are submitted, which determines the cash and margin available to the buy orders that follow in the same session, which changes which entries fill - and because every strategy is path dependent, that difference compounds for the rest of the year. Measured with the pre-fix code, same seed, same config, same machine: PYTHONHASHSEED=2 gave @OverreactionFade_LT +39.8994% on 71 closed trades; PYTHONHASHSEED=0, 1 and 3 gave +73.46% to +73.59% on 70 trades. The committed Season 1 memory had been written under whichever hash seed that process happened to get, so `python3 -m sim.cli run` could not reproduce the leaderboard the site was publishing. The in-process determinism test that had been passing all along could not catch this by construction: every object inside one interpreter shares one hash seed. The bug was invisible locally and only appeared when CI re-ran the season in a fresh process on a fresh runner.
  • https://docs.python.org/3/using/cmdline.html#envvar-PYTHONHASHSEED
  • https://docs.python.org/3/reference/datamodel.html#object.__hash__

Resolution: Fixed in sim/strategies.py: all three loops now iterate `ctx.symbols`, the canonical universe order, using the set only for membership. Verified across PYTHONHASHSEED 0, 1, 2, 3 and 7 - the leaderboard hash is now identical in all five processes. Two tests hold the line: tests/test_engine_smoke.py::test_the_season_is_reproducible_across_processes runs the short season in two subprocesses with different hash seeds and requires identical leaderboards (this is the test that would have caught it), and ::test_no_strategy_iterates_a_set is an AST sweep that fails on any `for` loop over a name bound to a set comprehension or set literal - both were checked against a deliberately reintroduced copy of the bug. The whole season was re-run and the site rebuilt after the fix, so the numbers published here are the deterministic ones. The remaining exposure is worth stating plainly: the fix removes hash-order sensitivity, not the underlying knife-edge behaviour measured in IR-29, which is a property of limit-order marketability and path dependence rather than of Python.

IR-31highDividends were paid on the position left AFTER the ex-date's trades, and short positions were never charged a manufactured dividendTwo accounting errors in one place, both found by reading sim/engine.py and sim/portfolio.py line by line in the second review pass rather than by running anything. (a) ENTITLEMENT. The engine settled an ex-date after the participant had already traded that day, paying the dividend on whatever the book looked like at the close. A dividend belongs to whoever held the shares going INTO the ex-date, so a participant who bought on the ex-date was paid a dividend they were never owed, and one who sold on the ex-date lost a dividend they were owed. Measured on the originally published Season 1 by re-deriving all 202 of its dividend events from the event streams: 18 of them were priced against a quantity that was not the entitlement carried into the ex-date - 11 overpayments totalling $1,198.01 and 7 underpayments totalling $962.48, $2,160.49 gross and -$235.53 net, across 7 of the 20 accounts. The largest single one paid @OvernightCarry_NO $547.68 of SPY dividend on 306 shares when it had carried 17 shares into the date; the smallest that mattered was $2.12, and it moved one participant 31.3 percentage points. (b) PAYMENT IN LIEU. A stock loan requires the borrower to pay the lender any dividend that goes ex while the shares are borrowed; the position does not receive the dividend, it owes one. pay_dividend() returned 0.0 for any non-positive quantity, so shorts paid nothing. The venue's own drift calibration makes this worse than a missing line item: for a simulated (non-endpoint-anchored) name the declared dividend yield is SUBTRACTED from the price drift, so the price path falls by roughly the dividend. A short therefore collected that fall as profit and was never charged for it - a free +yield tailwind on every short leg. That flattered exactly the short-heavy archetypes: the low-volatility pair trade, the market maker and the reversal book.
  • https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/4330
  • https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550
  • https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/stocks/xom/dividend-history

Resolution: sim/portfolio.py: pay_dividend() is replaced by settle_dividend(symbol, per_share, entitled_qty), which pays max(0, entitled_qty) * per_share to the long side and charges max(0, -entitled_qty) * per_share to the short side as a manufactured dividend, posting each as its own carry event so the audit trail shows the entitlement it used. sim/engine.py snapshots quantities_at_open() before ANY of the session's fills, including before a forced liquidation, and settles on that snapshot. The P&L identity and the decomposition gained a dividends_in_lieu_usd bucket, and the ledger-closure test now sums every *_usd bucket the decomposition publishes, so a future bucket cannot be added without being tested. Direct cash effect in the published season: the carry ledger went from $11,299.61 received and nothing charged to $11,600.83 received and $1,852.31 charged in lieu, a net correction of $1,551.09 across 20 accounts, about $78 each. Of that, $1,489.18 is attributable to this fix measured before IR-34 re-timed two books; the rest is those two books holding different quantities on ex-dates. An earlier draft of this entry quoted $635.91 over 4 payments, which could not be reproduced from any event stream in the repository; it was replaced by the derivation above rather than left standing, because an unverifiable figure in a document about verifiability is the same failure mode as IR-33's dead links, only on our own side. The effect on INDIVIDUAL RETURNS is much larger than that, and the reason is path dependence, not the size of the correction: @OverreactionFade_LT moves from +73.46% to +42.14% because a $2.12 cash difference on 2026-06-11 changed the size of a CVNA order one month later - the pre-fix book sold 94 shares where the corrected book sold 95 - and every later trade compounds from there. This is the same knife-edge that IR-29 measures, now demonstrated on an accounting input worth two dollars, which is a stronger argument than any of the synthetic perturbations: a single-season ranking in this venue is not a stable measurement of anything. Both fixes are guarded by new tests (test_dividend_entitlement_is_the_position_carried_into_the_ex_date, test_short_position_owes_a_manufactured_dividend, test_dividend_events_match_the_entitlement_recorded_at_the_open), each verified to fail against the pre-fix code. scripts/independent_audit.py was then added so the published numbers are re-derived from the event trail by code that never imports the engine.

IR-32mediumThe pattern-day-trader counter was a stub that returned True, so it counted any second fill of a session as a day tradeAccount._day_trade_closes(pos, side) had an unused signature and a body of 'return True'. Any symbol traded twice in one session was therefore recorded as a day trade, whatever the direction: three consecutive buys scale into a position and were counted as round-trip day trading. The published season had @OvernightCarry_NO at 235 'day trades' out of 251 sessions, for a strategy whose every round trip deliberately spans two sessions and which never round-trips intraday at all - the number was not merely imprecise, it described the strategy backwards. No P&L was affected, which is exactly why it survived: the rule is reported and not enforced at a $100,000 account, and the two tests that existed only pinned down 'one entry per session' and 'an overnight round trip is not a day trade', both of which the stub satisfied by accident.

  • https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/4210
  • https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/frequent-intraday-trading

Resolution: Replaced with real intraday tracking: the account keeps a per-symbol signed intraday quantity, reset at start_day(), and a day trade is counted only when a fill closes quantity opened the same session. Two counters are now reported - day_trade_count (round trips closed intraday) and day_trades (sessions containing at least one) - and the participant pages label the metric 'Intraday round trips (PDT definition)'. Adding to a position is verified not to count, a partial close is verified to count once, the short-side mirror (sell short then cover) is verified, and the counter is verified to reset across a session boundary. Confirmed to be purely informational: with the day-trade fix applied and the dividend fix reverted, the leaderboard reproduces the pre-fix committed bytes exactly.

IR-34highTwo strategies published exit rules the venue could not express, so they silently traded a different trade than they describedReading all twenty strategy implementations against their own specifications - the part of the brief that says the strategies must be faithful to the sources they were reverse-engineered from - @GapAndGo_YOLO documented 'Exit every position at the same session's close (market-on-close order)' and a thesis of being 'flat before the close so nothing is held overnight', while its code called ctx.flatten() once per session and every order the venue accepts was worked from interval 0, the opening bell. The fills prove what actually happened: on 2025-11-17 it sold 107 CVNA at interval 0 with the reason string 'close out yesterday's position at the open' and bought 5 shares in the same interval. So the strategy held every position overnight, earned the intraday leg it claimed to avoid, and paid the overnight gap risk it claimed to have closed out. @OvernightCarry_NO was the same defect in the opposite direction and, to its credit, had documented itself as understated (IR-08): a strategy whose whole edge is the overnight return was entering at the open, i.e. after the gap it exists to capture. Neither was a numerical bug; both were fidelity bugs, and they matter more than a rounding error would, because the site's central claim is that each username is a real strategy implemented honestly.

Sizing and the pre-trade margin check use the closing mark for such orders, because sizing an at-the-close order against the open price would silently produce a different position. Fill rows now carry at_close so the choice is visible in the audit trail. GapAndGo buys at the open and sells the same quantity at the close of that session, so it is flat by the bell for the first time in the project's history; OvernightCarry's entry moved to the close, which closes IR-08's understatement. A book that is genuinely flat by the close is never a holder of record on an ex-date, so it must earn nothing - the same property IR-31 was about, now confirmed from the opposite direction.
