Irregularities

Everything found during the build that did not behave the way a naive reading of the sources would predict - data quirks, regulation in transition, sources that could not be verified, and model choices where two defensible answers disagreed. Each one is recorded rather than quietly resolved, because a simulation that hides its own problems cannot be audited.

IR-01lowVIXCLS has observations on dates when SP500 does notThe two real FRED series used as market factors do not cover exactly the same dates. Over the Season 1 window, VIXCLS carries values on 7 dates where SP500 is blank. SP500 blanks are market closures (all ten cross-check against the published Nasdaq holiday schedule); VIX closes are nevertheless published on some of those dates because the VIX is computed from SPX option prices, and CBOE option settlement/holiday conventions do not line up one-for-one with the cash-index publication calendar. Taking the two series at face value would have produced a calendar with sessions the cash market never opened.
  • https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SP500
  • https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/VIXCLS
  • https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/stock-market-holiday-schedule

Resolution: The trading calendar is built from the SP500 series only. VIX observations on non-sessions are dropped and counted; the count is reported in the run manifest as vix_observations_dropped_on_closed_dates. Every session carries a real VIX value.

Working adapters for six providers are shipped so a season with real bars needs only credentials and network access.

IR-04lowThe 2024 Reg NMS half-penny tick tier is codified but exempt - verified, not assumedThe SEC's September 2024 Reg NMS amendments (Release 34-101070, published at 89 FR 81774) add a $0.005 minimum pricing increment for NMS stocks whose time-weighted average quoted spread is at or below $0.015, and cut the Rule 610(c) access-fee cap from $0.003 to $0.001 per share. That amended text IS in the current Code of Federal Regulations: 17 CFR 242.612(b)(2), retrieved from the eCFR on 2026-09-17 (title 17 up to date as of 2026-09-15), reads $0.01 where the TWAQS exceeded $0.015 and $0.005 where it did not. It is nevertheless NOT in force. The Commission granted a partial stay on 12-Dec-2024 (Release 34-101899) pending judicial review; the D.C. Circuit denied the petitions for review in October 2025; the Commission then granted temporary exemptive relief to the first business day of November 2026 on 31-Oct-2025; and Release 34-105656 (11-Jun-2026, published 15-Jun-2026 as document 2026-11997) extended that relief for Rules 600(b)(89)(i)(F), 610(c) and 612 until the first business day of November 2027. The entire Season 1 window (2025-09-17 to 2026-09-16) falls inside the exemption period. This entry was originally written as 'genuinely uncertain'; it is now resolved from primary sources and downgraded from medium to low.

IR-06mediumThe intraday-range calibration that best fits real SPY data contradicts the documented overnight/intraday variance splitA grid search over the wick coefficient and the share of daily variance placed in the overnight gap, scored against 13 real Yahoo SPY monthly bars, preferred wick=0.25 with overnight share=0.05 (mean absolute difference 0.401 percentage points). That overnight share is well below what the published literature on overnight versus intraday return variance supports, so the best-fitting point buys range accuracy by misrepresenting where variance occurs - and the overnight leg matters directly to one participant (@OvernightCarry_NO) and to every opening-gap decision.

Resolution: Compromise committed: overnight share 0.15, wick coefficient 0.20, mean absolute difference 0.522pp against the real monthly ranges. Both constants carry this note in marketdata.py, and the audit table is published on the data-provenance page so the cost of the compromise is visible.

IR-07highEvent-driven strategies run on price/volume proxies, not on real event dataTwo participants are built around information events: @DriftRider_PEAD (post-earnings-announcement drift) and @SqueezeHunter_TF (short-squeeze / low-float momentum). A proxy of that kind conflates genuine information events with ordinary volatility, and the two have opposite continuation behaviour.
  • https://doi.org/10.2307/2490899

Resolution: Both strategy specs declare this as a known failure mode in the first line, the narrative surfaces it as a standing caveat on every run, and the site repeats it on the participant pages. IR-08highThe overnight-premium participant is filled after the gap it is trying to capture@OvernightCarry_NO's edge is the overnight return: buy at the close, sell at the next open. A daily-bar engine decides at the open and executes from the open, so the participant systematically transacts after the very move it is trading. Its measured performance is therefore structurally biased against the strategy - or, on days when the gap reverses, in favour of it - and neither direction reflects the real overnight premium.

Resolution: Flagged in the strategy spec, in the narrative caveats and on the participant page. Fixing it properly needs a close-auction execution path (an order type that fills at the session close print), which is listed as remaining work.

IR-09lowEleven dividend schedules are declared approximations; only AAPL's are realAAPL carries four real ex-dates and amounts captured from the Yahoo chart API. The other dividend payers in the universe (SPY, QQQ, IWM, MSFT, GOOGL, META, NVDA, JPM, XOM, UNH, CAT, WMT) needed schedules too - without them the buy-and-hold control would be understated by roughly one dividend yield per year and that bias would leak into every relative comparison.

Resolution: Declared yields with quarterly ex-month patterns approximating each issuer's real cycle are attached and labelled 'scenario-declared' in the instrument provenance. To avoid double counting, the replay generator subtracts the declared window dividend yield from the price drift for non-anchored names, so declared total return equals price path plus cash dividends. Ex-dates falling on a non-session are paid on the next session and raise IR-10.

IR-14mediumRuntime: orders rejected by the broker-side pre-trade margin checkAn order that would breach the gross leverage cap and could not be resized (not even one share fits) is rejected, with the measured leverage in the reason. In the primary scenario @SpreadHarvester_MM had 131 such rejections: its inventory drifted the book above 2.0x gross through price movement, after which the broker only accepted risk-reducing orders.

Resolution: Aggregated per participant and per normalised reason so a single stuck book does not produce hundreds of near-identical flags. Risk-reducing orders are never blocked, so a participant can always trade its way back inside the cap.

IR-16highFactor premia measured on this replay are artefacts of declared parametersThe narrative engine attributes performance partly by comparing each strategy against realised long/short factor premia (momentum, reversal, beta, low-vol, liquidity) computed from the same replay. Those premia are real numbers about this competition, but the cross-sectional drifts that generate them are declared scenario inputs in sim/universe.py, not observed market data. IR-17lowThe SEC Section 31 fee rate was zero for the first two-thirds of the competition windowThe FY2026 annual adjustment order sets the Section 31 transaction fee rate at $0.00 per $1,000,000 from 2025-09-01 and $20.60 per $1,000,000 from 2026-04-04. A zero rate is unusual and is a consequence of the timing of appropriations and the Commission's fee-setting process, not of a policy decision to abolish the fee. A model that used a single constant rate across the window would misprice sells by the full amount for seven months.

Resolution: The cost model is date-dependent: rate_for() picks the rate in force on the trade date. Sell-side regulatory cost is therefore exactly zero before 2026-04-04 and $20.60 per million after it, matching the order.

IR-19mediumSingle-path cross-sectional dispersion is driven by idiosyncratic draws, not by a volatility-model errorIn the primary scenario the standard deviation of one-year returns across the 17 instruments is 47.3 percentage points, with NVDA +147%, JPM +60%, XOM -43% and UNH -52%. That is wide. Diagnosing it showed realised total volatility matching the predicted volatility closely for every name (NVDA 50.3% predicted vs 49.7% realised; JPM 22.5% vs 21.9%), so the vol model is correct and the spread is the expected consequence of cumulative idiosyncratic draws over one year (XOM z = -2.68, JPM z = +2.17, NVDA z = +1.63).

Resolution: The declared idiosyncratic volatilities were deliberately NOT shrunk to make one path look tidier - that would bias every result toward the mean. Instead the competition is run over six scenarios and the robustness panel reports mean, median, best and worst per participant, which is the honest way to show how much of a single-season rank is the draw.

IR-20highFound in review: a market-making participant could size quotes in the billions@SpreadHarvester_MM sized each resting quote at 10% of 21-day ADV without applying the inventory cap its own spec declares. On SPY that is roughly 6 million shares, about $3.9bn of notional, on a $100,000 account - and because the strategy constructed Order objects directly it bypassed the margin screen that Context.orders_to_targets applies. The account reached $140,652,977 of equity in six sessions.

Resolution: Two fixes, both committed. (1) The strategy now sizes min(10% of ADV, 60% of equity / price), matching its spec. (2) Defence in depth: the engine runs a broker-side pre-trade risk check on EVERY order regardless of how it was built, resizing to the largest permissible quantity or rejecting, and the event is raised as IR-14/IR-15. IR-21mediumFound in review: VWAP-slicing every market order handed participants free intraday driftMarket orders were spread across the whole session by volume weight, which is what a VWAP algorithm does, not what a market order does. Because the decision price is the opening mid, the difference between the session average price and the open was booked as favourable 'drift'. Resolution: Slicing is now capacity-driven: an order trades at the touch and only spans further intervals when its size exceeds what one interval can absorb inside the participation limit. IR-22mediumFound in review: round-trip reconstruction dropped the flipped leg of a position reversalWhen a fill took a position through zero (long 100 becomes short 200 on a 300-share sell), the trade ledger closed the original lot and discarded the residual 200 shares, so the entire P&L of the new position vanished from the attribution. Resolution: The reconstruction now opens a new lot in the opposite direction for the residual at the same fill price. IR-23mediumFound in review: beta was regressed one session out of alignmentThe equity curve holds one mark per session, so its daily-return series starts one session later than the market-return series it was paired with. The regression therefore compared each participant's return against the previous day's index move, which produced near-zero betas for obviously levered books (@BetaChaser_3xProxy measured beta 0.07 while carrying 2x gross in high-beta names) and inflated alphas to nonsense (+104.7% per year).

Resolution: Both series are now built over identical session ranges and length-checked before the regression. IR-24lowQuoted spreads from a pure volatility-scaled model were two orders of magnitude too wideAn early version derived the quoted spread from volatility (spread = 0.20 x daily sigma x price), which produced $0.51 on NVDA - 27 basis points. NVDA's real quoted spread is one cent, about 0.5bp. The error came from ignoring that liquid US equities are tick-constrained: competition compresses the touch to the minimum increment whenever the tick is small relative to the price, which is exactly why the SEC's 2024 amendments introduced a finer tick for the tightest names.
  • https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-242/section-242.612
  • https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-242/section-242.605

Resolution: The spread model is now tick-based: a whole number of Rule 612 increments, scaled by sqrt(sigma x sqrt(VIX factor) / (tick/price)) and clamped by per-liquidity-tier tick bounds plus an absolute basis-point cap. At the season open this gives 1 tick on SPY, NVDA, AAPL, JPM and RIVN and 2 ticks on TSLA and CVNA; all-in execution cost lands at 0.5bp for a $100k order in NVDA and about 10bp for 50,000 shares of RIVN, consistent with published execution-quality statistics.

IR-25mediumThe Commission has proposed rescinding the Rule 611 order protection rule, which this simulation assumesThe simulated order book never crosses and never trades through a better displayed price, because Rule 611 of Regulation NMS (17 CFR 242.611) makes the best protected quotation a trade-through protection point. On 11-Jun-2026, alongside the exemptive order that pushed the tick-size and access-fee amendments to November 2027, the Commission proposed rescinding the Order Protection Rule; Chairman Atkins' statement of the same date records that proposal and directs staff to review Rules 610(c) and 612 by the end of 2026. If Rule 611 is rescinded, displayed quotations would no longer be protected, off-exchange and manual execution would no longer have to route to the NBBO, and the price-improvement and trade-through assumptions baked into sim/microstructure.py would describe a market that no longer exists. Nothing in Season 1 is affected - the rule was in force for every session of the window - but any future season started after a rescission would need the venue model re-derived.
  • https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-statement-minimum-pricing-increments-access-fee-caps-061126
  • https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-242/section-242.611

Resolution: Flagged rather than modelled: Season 1 ran entirely under Rule 611 as it stands, so no result changes. The proposal is tracked here so a maintainer re-running a later season checks the status of Rule 611 first. If it is rescinded, the affected code is the no-cross / trade-through invariant in microstructure.OrderBook.match and the protected-quote assumption in the liquidity model's price-improvement statistics; both are documented inline with their Rule 611 citation.

IR-26mediumThe impact model uses a square-root exponent; the empirical paper it cites measured 3/5sim/microstructure.py prices temporary market impact as coefficient x sigma_daily x sqrt(shares / ADV), the square-root law. That functional form comes from the optimal-execution framework of Almgren & Chriss (2001), Journal of Risk 3(2):21-40. The empirical paper most often cited alongside it - Almgren, Thum, Hauptmann & Li (2005), 'Direct estimation of equity market impact', Risk 18(7):58-62, fitted to roughly 700,000 Citigroup US equity orders - explicitly REJECTS the square-root model for temporary impact as a function of trade rate and reports a 3/5 power law instead. Using 0.5 therefore understates the concavity of impact: at 10% of ADV the square-root form gives 0.316 sigma-units where a 0.6 exponent gives 0.251, i.e. this model charges about 26% MORE impact than the measured law at that size, and the gap widens for smaller orders and narrows for larger ones. Neither the publisher page for the 2005 article (checked 2026-09-17: risk.net returns 'we can't seem to find the page you're looking for') nor the author-hosted working paper (courant.nyu.edu, redirects to cims.nyu.edu and returns HTTP 403) was retrievable from this environment, so the exponent finding is cited from the literature rather than read from the paper here.
  • https://doi.org/10.21314/JOR.2001.041
  • https://www.risk.net/cut-through/2455921/direct-estimation-of-equity-market-impact

Resolution: The square-root form is retained deliberately: it is the convention every execution desk and vendor model uses, it keeps ImpactConfig.coefficient comparable across instruments, and switching exponents would change every cost number in Season 1 without a verified calibration to replace it. The deviation is documented here and in the ImpactConfig docstring. Making the exponent a config field (default 0.5, with a 0.6 scenario for sensitivity) is listed in research/REMAINING_WORK.json.

IR-28mediumDisplayed quotes were being re-centred off the Rule 612 tick grid, so the venue published prices no exchange could displayExecutionEngine.quote_at() builds the dealer ladder on the minimum-increment grid, then re-centres it on the permanently-impacted mid so that quotes track the intraday path. That re-centring added an arbitrary fraction (mid minus the raw ladder mid) to every level, which pushed the displayed bid and ask off the grid - the recorded opening quote for SPY on 2025-09-17 was bid 655.4003 / ask 655.4103, a perfectly legal one-tick spread at two prices that 17 CFR 242.612(b) does not permit anyone to display. Across the primary run this affected 8,462 of the recorded quote levels and 5,902 of the bid/ask fields carried on fill rows. Executed fill prices were NOT affected: the fill engine rounds to the grid separately, and all 2,973 fills in the primary run are exact multiples of the operative increment. The existing unit test did not catch it because it called MarketMakerPool.quotes() directly, which has always been grid-clean; the defect lived one level up, in the re-centring, and no test crossed that boundary. Found while writing the audit-trail test that checks the prices actually WRITTEN to memory rather than the prices the venue code computes in isolation.
  • https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-242/section-242.612
  • https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024-10-08/2024-21867/regulation-nms-minimum-pricing-increments-access-fees-and-transparency-of-better-priced-orders

Resolution: Fixed in sim/microstructure.py: the re-centring shift is now snapped to a whole number of minimum increments (round((mid - raw_mid) / tick) * tick), so the ladder still tracks the impacted mid to within half a tick, the quoted spread stays at exactly n_ticks, and the book cannot cross because every level moves by the same amount. Two tests now hold the line: tests/test_microstructure.py::test_every_displayed_level_is_a_legal_rule_612_increment walks every symbol and three intraday intervals and asserts each displayed level, the quoted spread and the mid-tracking bound; tests/test_memory.py::test_every_recorded_price_is_a_legal_rule_612_increment asserts it on the published audit trail itself, over every fill row and every quote row in memory. A companion test, test_the_decision_price_is_deliberately_off_grid, pins the other half of the distinction: the price a strategy decided at stays the unrounded intraday mark, so the tick cost is charged rather than handed back. Season 1 was re-run and the site rebuilt after the fix, so the numbers published here are the post-fix numbers.

Re-running the identical seed and config afterwards produced these differences against the pre-fix season: @OneBigBet_Concentra +28.3pp (20.6% to 48.9%) with closed trades rising from 36 to 55; @MeanRev_Z2Sigma +9.3pp; @IlliquidRocket_Degen -7.3pp; @SqueezeHunter_TF -5.6pp (crossing from +3.8% to -1.8%, i.e. changing verdict from 'made money but lagged the index' to 'lost money'); @DriftRider_PEAD +5.1pp; @KitchenSink_AllIn -4.3pp. Eleven of the twenty participants were unchanged to within 0.1pp. Nine of twenty changed rank, mostly by one place, at most by five. The mechanism is discrete, not continuous: whether a resting limit order is marketable depends on which side of the touch it sits, so moving the touch by a fraction of a tick flips fills on and off, and because every strategy is path dependent - position, inventory, subsequent signals, subsequent orders - one flipped fill compounds through the rest of the year. Trade counts changing (36 to 55) is the visible symptom: these are not the same trades at slightly different prices, they are different trades.

Resolution: Recorded rather than smoothed over, because it is a property of the design and not a bug to patch. Three consequences are now carried explicitly. (1) The season's ranking is sensitive to sub-tick venue detail, so no single-path ranking should be read as a skill ordering - this is exactly what the six-scenario robustness panel exists to answer, and it is why the site publishes mean, median, best, worst and standard deviation per participant rather than one number. (2) tests/fixtures.py pins the published headline in one place with a change history, so an engine change of any size shows up as a single explicit diff instead of a scattering of failing assertions. (3) A sensitivity harness is registered as remaining work: re-run the season under a small perturbation of each venue parameter (tick snapping, hidden liquidity, spread ticks, impact exponent) and publish the distribution of rank changes, so a reader can see which results are robust and which are knife-edge. Until that exists, the honest statement is the one on the site: one calendar path, six scenarios, one year, and no claim of statistical significance.
