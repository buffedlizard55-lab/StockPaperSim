Limitations and remaining work

What this project cannot do, why, and what would have to change. These are not caveats added for form: several of them materially bound how far any result on this site can be interpreted, and one of them (L-01) is the reason Season 1 is a replay at all.

Limitations (16)

L-01 · There is no live market-data feed, so Season 1 is a replay high

The build environment could reach pypi.org, files.pythonhosted.org and github.com directly. Every market-data host failed at the transport layer except FRED and the Yahoo v8 chart endpoint, which were reachable only through the page-fetch tool. A competition that advertises real-time prices cannot be run honestly under those conditions, so Season 1 replays a fixed, documented market path instead. This is the single largest limitation and the reason the site says 'simulated' as often as it says 'real'.

What would fix it: A vendor key plus outbound network access. Six adapters are already implemented and documented (Yahoo chart, Stooq, Alpaca, Polygon, Finnhub, Tiingo) and build_from_provider() will drive a season from any of them; the three marked real-time capable can support intraday polling.

L-02 · Single-name daily OHLCV is simulated, not observed high

Only the S&P 500 and VIX daily series are real observations across the whole window, plus 13 real SPY monthly bars and 5 real AAPL daily bars with 4 real AAPL dividends. Every other daily bar for every other instrument is generated from those real factors plus declared beta, idiosyncratic volatility and drift. Individual results are therefore conditional on design inputs that no vendor supplied.

What would fix it: Fetch real daily bars per symbol through a provider adapter (L-01). The generator is written so the replay can be swapped for observed bars without touching the engine, portfolio or analytics code.

L-03 · Decisions happen once per session, so intraday strategies are structurally mis-measured high

Strategies see prior-session history plus today's opening quote and act once. Three participants are built around intraday behaviour - @GapAndGo_YOLO (open-range momentum), @OvernightCarry_NO (close-to-open premium, see IR-08) and @SpreadHarvester_MM (passive quoting) - and for all three the engine's granularity, not the strategy's merit, dominates the result. A real market maker requotes in milliseconds; this one requotes daily. Their pages carry the caveat, but a caveat is not a measurement.

What would fix it: A minute-bar engine: intraday paths already exist internally (13 intervals per session), so the work is exposing multiple decision points per session, an order book that persists across them, and a close-auction order type.

L-04 · Each participant trades an independent replica of the venue medium

Impact and dealer inventory are per-participant, so one competitor's flow never changes another's fills. That makes the leaderboard independent of processing order and every run exactly reproducible, but it means the participants' combined flow does not clear a single shared book - in a real market, twenty aggressive participants trading the same names would move the price for each other.

What would fix it: A shared venue with a deterministic clearing step per interval, or an aggregate-flow impact term added to the replica mid. Either changes reproducibility guarantees and needs its own test suite.

L-05 · No corporate-event data of any kind high

There is no earnings calendar, no EPS surprise series, no short interest, no days-to-cover, no float, no news, no analyst revisions, no index reconstitution, no stock splits and no corporate actions beyond the declared cash dividends. Two strategies are forced onto price/volume proxies as a result (IR-07), and no participant can react to an event that is not in the price already.

What would fix it: Vendor event data. The cheapest high-value additions are an earnings-date calendar and FINRA short-interest files; both are published and machine-readable.

L-06 · Cash earns nothing and debit balances are financed only through declared borrow fees medium

Risk-free rate is zero throughout: Sharpe and Sortino are computed against zero, cash balances earn no interest, and margin debit balances are not charged a broker financing rate. Over a year at realistic 2025-2026 rates that is a material omission for levered participants, and it flatters every strategy that runs a cash deficit. It is also why the ratios are internally consistent - mixing a real T-bill rate into the numerator while paying no carry on cash would make them meaningless.

What would fix it: Add a financing curve (SOFR or T-bill plus a declared spread) to CostConfig, accrue it daily in Account.accrue_carry, and subtract it in the Sharpe numerator. Roughly a day of work once the rate source is chosen.

L-07 · Participants are strategies, not people medium

The competitions this project reverse-engineered track human traders who register, log in and click. StockPaperSim tracks Python classes with usernames. There is no authentication, no account creation, no order-entry UI and no self-registration, so the site is a results archive rather than a contest platform.

What would fix it: A web front end with auth, an order ticket that posts to a broker API backed by this engine, and per-user account state. The engine's per-participant venue replica design already supports adding participants at runtime.

L-08 · One competition year is one observation high

No ranking produced from a single 251-session path is statistically significant. A participant that finishes first may simply have held the name that drew well; the diagnostics showed exactly this mechanism at work (IR-19). The six-scenario robustness panel is a partial mitigation, not a substitute for many independent years, because all six share the same real index path.

What would fix it: More scenarios with independently drawn market factors, longer archives of real data, and formal significance testing (bootstrap confidence intervals on Sharpe and alpha; Deflated Sharpe Ratio after Bailey and Lopez de Prado) before any result is described as skill.

L-09 · The declared priors decide a large part of the outcome medium

Beta, idiosyncratic volatility, declared alpha, ADV, borrow availability and borrow fees per instrument are design inputs chosen to span a realistic cross-section. They are not vendor statistics and not forecasts. A strategy's rank is conditional on them: with different alphas, a different participant wins.

What would fix it: Publish the priors next to the results (the data-provenance page does exactly this, field by field), and re-run the season against priors estimated from real historical data once L-01 is resolved.

L-10 · Market microstructure is a stylised single-venue model medium

There is no venue fragmentation, no dark pools, no order routing, no market-on-close auction, no real queue-position tracking (resting limit fills use an approximate fill-ratio from how far the path traded through the limit), no iceberg detection, and one blended maker/taker rate instead of per-venue fee schedules. Displayed depth is a stylised ladder rather than observed Level II data.

What would fix it: Observed quote and depth data plus per-venue fee schedules; a queue-position model calibrated against real fill rates for resting orders; an explicit closing-auction mechanism.

L-11 · Margin is a simplified Reg T plus a house maintenance rule low

Initial margin 50% per Reg T, a 2.0x gross cap and a 30% maintenance requirement are SIM CHOICES - The Leap allows only 1:1 on stocks. There is no special memorandum account, no house-call sequencing, no portfolio margin, and the pattern-day-trader rule is counted and reported but not enforced (these are paper accounts with no real broker behind them).

What would fix it: An SMA-based equity computation with house-call sequencing, and PDT enforcement that restricts a participant to closing transactions for five sessions after the fourth day trade in a rolling five-day window below $25,000.

L-12 · Two fee inputs rest on secondary sources or uncertain effective dates low

The FINRA Trade Activity Fee rate is corroborated only from broker documentation (IR-05), and whether the SEC's 2024 half-penny tick tier and $0.001 access-fee cap are operative during the window could not be settled from primary sources (IR-04). Both are small in dollar terms but both are regulatory facts that should be cited to the regulator, not to a broker.

What would fix it: Retrieve FINRA Schedule A and the current compliance-date order for the 2024 Reg NMS amendments, then pin both constants to the primary text.

L-13 · The site is static and does not update itself low

docs/ is generated by scripts/build_site.py from run memory and published as-is. Nothing refreshes on a schedule, there is no live leaderboard feed and no intraday snapshot - unlike the referenced competitions, whose leaderboards update as often as hourly.

What would fix it: A scheduled GitHub Actions job that re-runs the season and republishes, or a small API service serving the memory store with the site fetching it client-side.

L-14 · Memory is compressed JSON Lines, not a database low

Queries are streaming scans over gzip files. At the current scale (about 38,000 event rows and 4.3 MB for six scenarios) that is instant and has the advantage of being human-readable, dependency-free and trivially checksummable. It would not survive 10 million rows or concurrent writers.

What would fix it: A SQLite or DuckDB backend behind the same MemoryStore interface; the JSONL files stay as the export format. The query API is already written against an iterator abstraction, so the swap is local.

L-15 · No strategy can be added without editing Python medium

The roster is a list of classes in sim/strategies.py. Adding a participant means writing code, which rules out the thing a competition platform exists for: letting people bring their own ideas.

What would fix it: A declarative strategy format (JSON or a small DSL) interpreted by the engine, with the existing classes as reference implementations, plus validation that a submitted strategy cannot see future data.

L-16 · Dividends are paid without a matching ex-date price drop low

Real price series gap down on the ex-date; the simulated series do not. For non-anchored names the generator compensates by removing the declared window dividend yield from the price drift, so total return is not double counted, but the day-to-day price path does not show the ex-date step that a real chart would. AAPL is exempt because its anchor is a real unadjusted close that already contains the real drops.

What would fix it: Apply an explicit multiplicative ex-date adjustment to the simulated close series on each declared ex-date and stop subtracting the yield from drift.

What success would require

  1. A real feed. Everything downstream of L-01 - real-time prices, real spreads, real depth, real short interest, a real earnings calendar - is blocked on a market-data vendor key and a host the runtime can reach. The adapters are written and tested against their documented response shapes; what is missing is network access and credentials.
  2. Intraday granularity. Three participants (gap-and-go, overnight carry, market making) are structurally mis-measured by a daily-bar engine, and their pages say so. Minute bars would make them meaningful.
  3. More seasons. One year is one draw. The robustness panel is a partial answer; the real answer is many independent years, which needs the real feed above or a longer historical archive.
  4. Human participants. This site tracks simulated strategies. The competitions it was reverse-engineered from track people, which needs accounts, authentication and an order-entry UI - none of which is in scope here.

StockPaperSim is a research and education project. Every price series a participant traded on is either a real published observation (FRED S&P 500 and VIX closes, Yahoo Finance monthly SPY bars and AAPL bars/dividends) or an explicitly labelled simulation of it. Nothing here is investment advice, and no result on this site should be read as evidence about a strategy's real future performance. See Limitations.

Built from run memory by scripts/build_site.py. Data integrity: every file is SHA-256 checksummed in its run manifest.
