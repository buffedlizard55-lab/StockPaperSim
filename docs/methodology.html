Methodology

How a session is simulated, what is real, what is modelled, and which choices were judgement calls. Every SIM CHOICE below is also written into the source with the reason and, where one exists, the citation.

2 · The market each participant trades

  • Real factors. The market return series is FRED SP500; the volatility regime is FRED VIXCLS. Both are stored verbatim under data/real/fred/.
  • Real anchors. SPY is derived from the real index level through a ratio fitted on 13 real Yahoo monthly bars (max absolute error 0.145%); AAPL starts and ends on real observed closes and pays four real dividends.
  • Simulated single-name paths. Each name's daily log return is beta × market log return + idiosyncratic shock, where the shock scales with the real VIX relative to its window mean and is drawn from a fat-tail mixture (4% of draws scaled ×2.6, renormalised to unit variance) after Cont (2001).
  • Intraday shape. Each daily bar is expanded into 13 intervals that visit the open, high, low and close, with a U-shaped volume profile after Admati & Pfleiderer (1988). Overnight gap share 15% of daily variance; wick size 0.20 × daily sigma.
  • Calendar. Sessions and closures come from the real FRED series' own gaps, cross-checked against the Nasdaq holiday schedule; early closes (13:00 ET) are modelled as shorter sessions with proportionally lower volume.

3 · Liquidity, quoting and market making

  • Quoted spread. A whole number of Rule 612 minimum increments, because that is what actually binds for liquid US equities: ticks = clamp(round(0.05 × sqrt(sigma_daily × sqrt(vix_factor) / (tick / price))), min, max) with per-liquidity-tier bounds {"mega": 1, "large": 1, "mid": 1, "small": 1, "micro": 2} … {"mega": 3, "large": 4, "mid": 6, "small": 12, "micro": 30} and an absolute cap in basis points. Result at the season open: 1 tick on SPY, NVDA, AAPL, JPM, RIVN; 2 ticks on TSLA and CVNA.
  • Depth. A 6-level ladder, displayed size 20 round lots at the touch growing ×1.60 per level, with round lots taken from the tiered Rule 600(b)(93) definition.
  • Market makers. 4 competing dealers per instrument per session quote around an Avellaneda-Stoikov reservation price, so inventory skews the ladder away from flow (Avellaneda & Stoikov 2008). Passive orders earn a $0.0020/share rebate; aggressive orders pay $0.0030/share, at the Rule 610 access-fee cap.
  • Non-displayed liquidity. Quantity beyond the displayed ladder fills at the touch and is priced by the impact model rather than by walking an artificially deep book, because roughly 45% of consolidated US equity volume trades off-exchange (FINRA OTC Transparency, cited but not fetched here).
  • Book integrity. The displayed book may never cross; a defensive uncross step enforces it, as Rule 611 trade-through protection implies.

4 · Execution

  • Order types. Market, limit (marketable or resting, with a queue-position fill model) and stop (triggered off the intraday path, then executed as a market order from the trigger interval).
  • Slicing. Capacity-driven, not time-driven: an order trades at the touch and only spans further intervals when its size exceeds what one interval can absorb inside the 10% participation limit. An earlier version spread every market order across the whole session (a VWAP algorithm), which handed every participant a free intraday average price; measured on one participant it was worth −$1,857 of phantom “drift benefit” on $614k of paper notional, and it was removed.
  • Impact. Square-root law, 0.55 × sigma_daily × sqrt(shares / ADV), split 35% permanent / 65% temporary, with 50% of the permanent component decaying by the next session (Almgren & Chriss 2001; empirical calibration from Almgren, Thum, Hauptmann & Li 2005, Risk 18(7):58-62 - see IR-26).
  • Fees are dated schedules, not constants. SEC §31 on sells: $0.00 per $1,000,000 of sell notional from 2025-09-01 · $20.60 per $1,000,000 of sell notional from 2026-04-04, per the FY2026 annual adjustment order. FINRA Trade Activity Fee on sells: $0.000166 per share from 2025-01-01 · $0.000195 per share from 2026-01-01, capped at $8.30 per trade from 2025-01-01 · $9.79 per trade from 2026-01-01 - rate and cap from Section 1 of Schedule A to the FINRA By-Laws (2026 version) and, for the 2025 rate, from the Commission's publication of SR-FINRA-2024-019. Both schedules were SECONDARY-sourced until 2026-09-18; IR-05 is closed and the numbers did not move. The earliest date any of these schedules is documented for is 2025-09-01; config.validate_fee_coverage() refuses to run a season that opens before it, so no trade is ever costed at a rate this project could not cite. Commission $0.00 per order and $0.0000 per share by default. Exchange access fees are capped at $0.0030 per share under Rule 610.
  • Short selling. Requires a locate: names flagged unborrowable are rejected outright, and borrowable names accrue a daily fee at their declared annual rate (Regulation SHO Rule 203(b)).
  • Pre-trade risk. Every order - including ones a strategy built by hand - is screened by the broker before it reaches the venue. Orders that would breach the 2.0x gross cap are resized down to the largest permissible quantity, or rejected if not even one share fits. Orders that strictly reduce a position are never blocked, so a book that drifts above the cap through price movement can still be trimmed.

5 · Accounting

  • Average-cost basis per instrument; realised P&L on reduction, unrealised on the mark; cash dividends on ex-dates; borrow fees daily on shorts.
  • Maintenance margin tested at every close. A breach is liquidated at the next open (a daily-bar engine cannot liquidate intraday), and the event is written to memory.
  • Day trades counted for pattern-day-trader visibility ($25,000 threshold), reported but not enforced: these are paper accounts.

6 · Analysis

  • Returns, CAGR, max drawdown (with peak/trough/recovery dates and sessions underwater), Sharpe and Sortino (Sharpe 1966, Sortino & Price 1994), Calmar, historical VaR/CVaR at 95% and 99% (Artzner et al. 1999), skew and excess kurtosis.
  • Beta, alpha, R² and information ratio from a daily-return OLS on the real index return, aligned session-for-session.
  • Trade reconstruction by average-cost round trip, including positions that flip through zero (an earlier version dropped the flipped leg entirely and lost $20,169 of one participant's P&L from the attribution).
  • Implementation shortfall decomposed into spread, depth, impact, fees, intraday drift and missed trades (Perold 1988). The components are mutually exclusive by construction: for every fill, spread + depth + impact + drift equals the signed price difference against the decision mid, so the total cannot double count.
  • Rule-based post-mortems. Each sentence in a narrative fires only on a measured quantity; the strategy's declared priors are used afterwards to label the causes the numbers already identified, never to produce them.

7 · Determinism and memory

  • Every random draw is keyed by an explicit string (seed, participant, symbol, date, order attributes) - never by wall-clock time or hash ordering. Two runs with the same seed produce byte-identical memory.
  • Each participant trades against an independent replica of the venue, so one participant's impact and dealer inventory cannot change another's fills and the leaderboard does not depend on processing order. The trade-off - participants' combined flow never clears one shared book - is limitation L-04.
  • Every order, fill, rejection, quote snapshot, daily mark, position snapshot, carry payment and margin event is written to memory/runs/<run_id>/ as gzip-compressed JSON Lines, with a SHA-256 checksum per file and the git commit plus per-module source hashes in the run manifest. python3 -m sim.cli verify re-hashes the lot.

python3 -m sim.cli sources             # the verified-source register
python3 scripts/build_site.py          # regenerate this site into docs/
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v

StockPaperSim is a research and education project. Every price series a participant traded on is either a real published observation (FRED S&P 500 and VIX closes, Yahoo Finance monthly SPY bars and AAPL bars/dividends) or an explicitly labelled simulation of it. Nothing here is investment advice, and no result on this site should be read as evidence about a strategy's real future performance. See Limitations.
