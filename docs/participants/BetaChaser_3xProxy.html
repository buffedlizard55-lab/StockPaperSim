Post-mortem: why it worked, or why it did not

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@BetaChaser_3xProxy (Beta Chaser) ran the "levered beta" archetype. Declared thesis: In a rising market the highest-beta names deliver the highest return. This participant is the human equivalent of a 3x levered ETF: hold the top-beta basket at maximum permitted leverage and rebalance weekly. Entry: Rank the universe by realised beta to the S&P 500 over 63 sessions; Hold the top 5 beta names equally weighted at 2.0x gross; Re-rank weekly. Exit: Only at the weekly rebalance; never de-risks on a drawdown. Sizing: 40% of equity per name = 200% gross. Leverage: 2.0x gross, always invested. Cadence: weekly, horizon all season. Aggression 5/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Maximum market exposure at all times: if the market goes up, this participant should win.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: +108.39% over 251 sessions - +108,393 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 208,393. Verdict: beat the market.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The beta premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+39.73% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path. The market premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+14.42% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the +108.39% total, about +56.52pp is explained by carrying beta 3.92 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.50); the residual +51.87pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. CVNA contributed +51,526 USD (48% of net P&L) across 22 closed trades; RIVN was the biggest drag at -1,427 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 240 USD (0.24% of starting capital, 1.6 bp per fill, 1.8 bp of the 1,351,838 USD traded). 71% of that was spread/depth/impact and 29% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

66 closed round trips, 77.3% winners, profit factor 7.69, average winner +2,440 vs average loser -1,079 USD, median holding 158 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 251 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 10040 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: RIVN long 2025-09-17 to 2026-09-16, -8,790 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -43.52% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2026-03-27, recovered 2026-04-17. Average gross exposure 330% of starting capital, peak leverage 2.26x. Daily returns were +0.10 skewed with excess kurtosis +0.52; historical 95% VaR -6.03%/day with CVaR -8.94%/day. Worst single session -12.78%, best +16.95%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 35.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

Declared failure mode(s) that actually fired: Volatility decay: repeated rebalancing to a fixed leverage in a choppy market bleeds value; A 2x gross book in a 10% market drawdown is a 20% account drawdown with no brake.

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.

Answer: did it work, and why

It worked: it beat the index by 93.98pp and the excess survives a beta adjustment (+51.87pp residual), so the outperformance is not simply leverage on a rising market. Finished at +108.39% against the S&P 500's +14.41% over the same 251 sessions - an excess return of +93.98pp.
