Post-mortem: why it worked, or why it did not

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@Contrarian_DeepValue (Contrarian Deep Value) ran the "long-horizon reversal" archetype. Declared thesis: The market over-reacts to sustained bad news. Buy the three worst 126-session performers at 2x gross and hold them, betting on the long-horizon reversal that De Bondt and Thaler documented. Entry: Rank the universe by trailing 126-session return (or all available history); Buy the bottom 3 at 66% of equity each; Re-rank quarterly. Exit: Quarterly re-rank only. Sizing: 66% of equity per name = 200% gross. Leverage: 2.0x gross. Cadence: quarterly, horizon 3-12 months. Aggression 4/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Buys the most hated names at double leverage because that is where the reversal payoff is largest.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: +18.88% over 251 sessions - +18,877 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 118,877. Verdict: roughly matched the market.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The reversal premium went against the declared exposure (-6.01% for a +1.0 loading) yet the account still returned +18.88% - the result was driven by something other than this factor. The momentum premium went against the declared exposure (+3.82% for a -1.0 loading) yet the account still returned +18.88% - the result was driven by something other than this factor. The low_vol premium went against the declared exposure (-21.86% for a +0.5 loading) yet the account still returned +18.88% - the result was driven by something other than this factor.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the +18.88% total, about +40.03pp is explained by carrying beta 2.78 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.49); the residual -21.15pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. MSFT contributed +17,109 USD (91% of net P&L) across 1 closed trades; TSLA was the biggest drag at -85 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 21 USD (0.02% of starting capital, 0.5 bp per fill, 0.5 bp of the 416,182 USD traded). 62% of that was spread/depth/impact and 38% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

3 closed round trips, 66.7% winners, profit factor 202.64, average winner +8,602 vs average loser -85 USD, median holding 250 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 251 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 750 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: TSLA long 2025-09-17 to 2026-09-16, -85 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -51.15% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2026-03-27, never recovered. Average gross exposure 211% of starting capital, peak leverage 2.46x. Daily returns were +0.09 skewed with excess kurtosis +0.51; historical 95% VaR -5.42%/day with CVaR -6.74%/day. Worst single session -8.84%, best +10.02%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 35.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

Declared failure mode(s) that actually fired: 2x leverage on falling knives accelerates the drawdown.

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.

Answer: did it work, and why

It beat the index by 4.46pp, but after beta the residual is -21.15pp: the win came from carrying more market risk (beta 2.78) in an up market, not from the signal itself. Finished at +18.88% versus the S&P 500's +14.41% - effectively in line with simply holding the index.

Declared failure modes: what fired, and what this run cannot test

Actually fired (measured on this run)

Cannot be tested by this simulation

  • A 12-month window is far shorter than the 3-5 years the effect is documented over
  • The worst performers are often worst for fundamental reasons (value traps)
