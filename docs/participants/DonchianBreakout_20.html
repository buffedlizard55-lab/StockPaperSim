Post-mortem: why it worked, or why it did not

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@DonchianBreakout_20 (Donchian Breakout) ran the "channel breakout" archetype. Declared thesis: A close above the 20-session high means the market has accepted a price nobody was willing to sell at for a month. Breakouts from consolidation continue often enough, and when they do they run far, to pay for the many false ones. Entry: Enter long when the prior close is above the prior 20-session high; Enter short when the prior close is below the prior 20-session low; Maximum 4 concurrent positions. Exit: Exit longs on a close below the 10-session low; exit shorts on a close above the 10-session high. Sizing: 25% of equity per position, up to 100% gross long plus 100% gross short. Leverage: up to 2.0x gross. Cadence: daily, horizon days to weeks. Aggression 4/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Cuts losers mechanically and lets a genuine breakout run without a profit cap.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: +18.23% over 251 sessions - +18,232 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 118,232. Verdict: roughly matched the market.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The momentum premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+3.82% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path. The reversal premium this participant is built to harvest was live (-6.01% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the +18.23% total, about +22.67pp is explained by carrying beta 1.57 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.34); the residual -4.43pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. CVNA contributed +13,135 USD (72% of net P&L) across 1 closed trades; UNH was the biggest drag at -15,297 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 51 USD (0.05% of starting capital, 1.3 bp per fill, 1.2 bp of the 404,970 USD traded). 67% of that was spread/depth/impact and 33% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

8 closed round trips, 50.0% winners, profit factor 2.00, average winner +8,967 vs average loser -4,477 USD, median holding 247 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 251 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 1764 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: UNH long 2025-09-18 to 2026-09-16, -15,297 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -19.44% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2026-09-16, never recovered. Average gross exposure 181% of starting capital, peak leverage 1.78x. Daily returns were -0.20 skewed with excess kurtosis -0.16; historical 95% VaR -3.78%/day with CVaR -4.57%/day. Worst single session -5.58%, best +5.66%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 35.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

None of the failure modes declared in the strategy spec fired in this window.

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.

Answer: did it work, and why

It beat the index by 3.82pp, but after beta the residual is -4.43pp: the win came from carrying more market risk (beta 1.57) in an up market, not from the signal itself. Finished at +18.23% versus the S&P 500's +14.41% - effectively in line with simply holding the index.
