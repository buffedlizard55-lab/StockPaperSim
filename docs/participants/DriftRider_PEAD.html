Post-mortem: why it worked, or why it did not

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@DriftRider_PEAD (Drift Rider) ran the "post-event drift" archetype. Declared thesis: Markets digest a large information shock over days, not seconds. When a name moves more than 8% in five sessions on at least twice its normal volume, the move continues in the same direction for weeks - the drift that earnings announcement research documents. Entry: 5-session absolute return > 8%; 20-session average volume on the event day >= 2.0x the 63-session average; Go with the direction of the move, up to 5 names, ranked by |move|. Exit: Hold 15 sessions then exit; Exit early if the move retraces more than 60%. Sizing: equal 35% of equity per name = up to 175% gross. Leverage: up to 1.75x gross. Cadence: daily scan, horizon 15 sessions. Aggression 4/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Concentrates into the strongest information event in the universe and refuses to take profit early.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: +20.70% over 251 sessions - +20,702 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 120,702. Verdict: beat the market.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The momentum premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+3.82% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the +20.70% total, about +3.36pp is explained by carrying beta 0.23 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.02); the residual +17.34pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. UNH contributed +11,869 USD (57% of net P&L) across 4 closed trades; META was the biggest drag at -8,630 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 353 USD (0.35% of starting capital, 1.6 bp per fill, 1.7 bp of the 2,095,990 USD traded). 75% of that was spread/depth/impact and 25% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

40 closed round trips, 45.0% winners, profit factor 1.54, average winner +3,349 vs average loser -1,777 USD, median holding 15 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 244 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 1003 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: GOOGL short 2026-03-23 to 2026-04-09, -5,325 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -14.80% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2026-04-09, recovered 2026-07-02. Average gross exposure 47% of starting capital, peak leverage 1.67x. Daily returns were +0.04 skewed with excess kurtosis +3.13; historical 95% VaR -2.48%/day with CVaR -3.65%/day. Worst single session -5.98%, best +5.62%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 35.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

None of the failure modes declared in the strategy spec fired in this window.

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

Standing limitations of this simulation that apply to this participant regardless of the regime: PROXY DATA LIMITATION: no earnings calendar or EPS surprise series is available offline, so the entry uses a price/volume proxy rather than a real announcement (flagged as IR-07) The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.

Answer: did it work, and why

It worked: it beat the index by 6.29pp and the excess survives a beta adjustment (+17.34pp residual), so the outperformance is not simply leverage on a rising market. Finished at +20.70% against the S&P 500's +14.41% over the same 251 sessions - an excess return of +6.29pp.

Sizing: equal 35% of equity per name = up to 175% gross · Leverage: up to 1.75x gross · Cadence: daily scan · Horizon: 15 sessions
