Post-mortem: why it worked, or why it did not

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@GapAndGo_YOLO (Gap And Go) ran the "intraday momentum" archetype. Declared thesis: A stock that gaps up on the open has absorbed news or imbalanced overnight order flow; continuation in the first hours is common because retail and institutional demand arrives through the session. Take the continuation and be flat before the close so nothing is held overnight. Entry: At the open, compute gap = open / prior close - 1; Buy any name gapping up more than +1.5%; Skip names whose quoted spread is wider than 30 bp (costs eat the edge). Exit: Exit every position at the same session's close, as a market-on-close order routed to the venue's final interval; No stop loss intraday; Any exit that fails to fill at the close is flattened at the next open. Sizing: equal 40% of equity across qualifying names, capped at 4 names = up to 160% gross. Leverage: up to 1.6x gross. Cadence: daily, horizon intraday. Aggression 5/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Turns the whole account over every day, so a small per-trade edge compounds ~250 times a year.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: -91.75% over 251 sessions - -91,754 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 8,246. Verdict: lost money.
