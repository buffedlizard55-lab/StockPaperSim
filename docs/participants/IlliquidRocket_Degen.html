Post-mortem: why it worked, or why it did not

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@IlliquidRocket_Degen (Illiquid Rocket) ran the "liquidity-constrained aggression" archetype. Declared thesis: The highest theoretical returns live in the least liquid names. This participant deliberately trades the two smallest dollar-volume names in the universe at maximum leverage, chasing 5-session momentum. It exists to measure exactly how much return the microstructure model takes away. Entry: Rank the universe by dollar volume, take the two smallest; Go 2x gross into whichever of the two has the better 5-session momentum; Rebalance every 5 sessions. Exit: Weekly rotation only. Sizing: 200% of equity in a single name. Leverage: 2.0x gross, single name. Cadence: weekly, horizon 5 sessions. Aggression 5/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Concentrates everything into the highest-volatility names the competition allows.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: +15.80% over 251 sessions - +15,801 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 115,801. Verdict: roughly matched the market.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The liquidity premium this participant is built to harvest was live (-10.84% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path. The beta premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+39.73% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the +15.80% total, about +14.67pp is explained by carrying beta 1.02 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.02); the residual +1.13pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. CVNA contributed +24,039 USD (152% of net P&L) across 6 closed trades; RIVN was the biggest drag at -20,079 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 1,865 USD (1.87% of starting capital, 5.1 bp per fill, 5.3 bp of the 3,514,352 USD traded). 89% of that was spread/depth/impact and 11% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

13 closed round trips, 53.8% winners, profit factor 1.29, average winner +10,151 vs average loser -9,210 USD, median holding 15 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 251 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 549 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: RIVN long 2025-10-09 to 2025-10-29, -28,778 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -45.05% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2025-10-16, recovered 2025-12-05. Average gross exposure 65% of starting capital, peak leverage 2.69x. Daily returns were -0.32 skewed with excess kurtosis +9.33; historical 95% VaR -8.07%/day with CVaR -14.82%/day. Worst single session -26.74%, best +28.25%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 35.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

Declared failure mode(s) that actually fired: Single-name concentration at 2x means one bad week can halve the account.

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.

Answer: did it work, and why

It worked: it beat the index by 1.39pp and the excess survives a beta adjustment (+1.13pp residual), so the outperformance is not simply leverage on a rising market. Finished at +15.80% versus the S&P 500's +14.41% - effectively in line with simply holding the index.
