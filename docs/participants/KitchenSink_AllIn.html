Post-mortem: why it worked, or why it did not

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@KitchenSink_AllIn (Kitchen Sink) ran the "signal stacking" archetype. Declared thesis: Stack the signals instead of choosing between them. A name that is simultaneously above its 50-session EMA, inside 5% of its 63-session high, positive on 21-session momentum and in a rising-market regime gets the full 2x allocation. Names that fail any test get nothing. Entry: EMA20 > EMA50 on prior closes; Close within 5% of the 63-session high; 21-session momentum > 0; Index above its 50-session mean (regime filter); Equal weight across all qualifying names, scaled to 2.0x gross. Exit: A name is dropped the moment it fails any test; The whole book goes to cash if the regime filter fails. Sizing: 2.0x gross divided equally across qualifying names. Leverage: 2.0x gross or 0x. Cadence: daily, horizon days to weeks. Aggression 4/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Refuses to hold anything that is not confirming on every axis, then sizes the survivors to the cap.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: +5.19% over 251 sessions - +5,187 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 105,187. Verdict: made money but lagged the index.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The momentum premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+3.82% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path. The beta premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+39.73% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path. The reversal premium this participant is built to harvest was live (-6.01% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the +5.19% total, about +22.44pp is explained by carrying beta 1.56 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.32); the residual -17.26pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. CVNA contributed +15,482 USD (298% of net P&L) across 21 closed trades; NVDA was the biggest drag at -6,759 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 385 USD (0.39% of starting capital, 0.5 bp per fill, 0.5 bp of the 7,522,197 USD traded). 72% of that was spread/depth/impact and 28% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

354 closed round trips, 67.8% winners, profit factor 1.05, average winner +332 vs average loser -666 USD, median holding 14 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 251 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 9169 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: NVDA long 2026-09-14 to 2026-09-16, -5,855 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -30.49% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2026-04-23, recovered 2026-08-04. Average gross exposure 164% of starting capital, peak leverage 2.14x. Daily returns were +0.07 skewed with excess kurtosis +2.66; historical 95% VaR -3.55%/day with CVaR -5.32%/day. Worst single session -7.86%, best +9.31%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 35.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

Declared failure mode(s) that actually fired: The regime filter is a lagging index average, so the book goes to cash after the drop, not before.

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.

Answer: did it work, and why

It made money (+5.19%) but under-delivered versus simply holding the index (+14.41%); the signal did not pay for its own costs, drawdown and turnover. Finished at +5.19%, profitable but 9.23pp behind the S&P 500's +14.41%.
