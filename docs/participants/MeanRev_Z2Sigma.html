Post-mortem: why it worked, or why it did not

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@MeanRev_Z2Sigma (Mean Rev Z2) ran the "short-term reversal" archetype. Declared thesis: Liquidity provision is paid for. A large-cap that trades two standard deviations below its own 20-session mean in a single session has usually been hit by transient order flow, and it snaps back within days. Entry: Compute the z-score of yesterday's close against its 20-session mean; Buy names with z <= -2.0; Rank by most negative z, take up to 6 names; Skip names whose quoted spread exceeds 50 bp. Exit: Exit when z returns to >= -0.25 (back at the mean); Exit unconditionally after 10 sessions. Sizing: equal 30% of equity per name = up to 180% gross. Leverage: up to 1.8x gross. Cadence: daily, horizon 1-10 sessions. Aggression 4/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Buys the panic and is paid the liquidity premium, with turnover high enough to compound the edge.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: +74.85% over 251 sessions - +74,847 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 174,847. Verdict: beat the market.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The reversal premium went against the declared exposure (-6.01% for a +1.0 loading) yet the account still returned +74.85% - the result was driven by something other than this factor. The momentum premium went against the declared exposure (+3.82% for a -0.5 loading) yet the account still returned +74.85% - the result was driven by something other than this factor.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the +74.85% total, about +7.36pp is explained by carrying beta 0.51 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.16); the residual +67.48pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. CVNA contributed +26,079 USD (35% of net P&L) across 4 closed trades; JPM was the biggest drag at -4,602 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 242 USD (0.24% of starting capital, 0.9 bp per fill, 0.9 bp of the 2,680,990 USD traded). 76% of that was spread/depth/impact and 24% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

53 closed round trips, 79.2% winners, profit factor 8.42, average winner +2,022 vs average loser -917 USD, median holding 8 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 251 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 2822 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: JPM long 2026-03-09 to 2026-03-23, -2,395 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -7.01% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2026-03-26, recovered 2026-04-30. Average gross exposure 34% of starting capital, peak leverage 1.78x. Daily returns were +2.12 skewed with excess kurtosis +13.58; historical 95% VaR -0.79%/day with CVaR -1.56%/day. Worst single session -4.95%, best +7.14%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 35.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

None of the failure modes declared in the strategy spec fired in this window.

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.

Answer: did it work, and why

It worked: it beat the index by 60.43pp and the excess survives a beta adjustment (+67.48pp residual), so the outperformance is not simply leverage on a rising market. Finished at +74.85% against the S&P 500's +14.41% over the same 251 sessions - an excess return of +60.43pp.

Declared failure modes: what fired, and what this run cannot test

Actually fired (measured on this run)

None.

Cannot be tested by this simulation

  • Catching a falling knife: a genuine fundamental break keeps falling
  • The 2-sigma filter fires most often exactly when volatility is rising
  • Exits are limit orders at the mean, so the best days can fail to fill
