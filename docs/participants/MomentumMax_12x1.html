Post-mortem: why it worked, or why it did not

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@MomentumMax_12x1 (Momentum Max) ran the "cross-sectional momentum" archetype. Declared thesis: Rank the universe by trailing 63-session return with the most recent 5 sessions skipped, buy the top quartile and hold for a month. Winners keep winning because information diffuses slowly and investors under-react then herd. Entry: At the first session of each month, rank all 17 instruments by 63-session return, skipping the last 5 sessions; Buy the top 4 names in equal weight; Skip any name whose 63-session average dollar volume is below $100m (untradeable size). Exit: Rebalance to the new top 4 at the next month turn; No stop loss: the position is only exited at the rebalance. Sizing: equal weight, 50% of equity per name = 200% gross. Leverage: 2.0x gross (Reg T initial margin 50%). Cadence: monthly, horizon 1 month. Aggression 4/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Momentum has one of the largest documented premia in equities; levered and concentrated it is the highest-expected-return sleeve we can build from a single signal.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: +51.21% over 251 sessions - +51,214 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 151,214. Verdict: beat the market.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The momentum premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+3.82% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path. The reversal premium this participant is built to harvest was live (-6.01% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path. The beta premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+39.73% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the +51.21% total, about +46.85pp is explained by carrying beta 3.25 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.56); the residual +4.37pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. NVDA contributed +22,258 USD (43% of net P&L) across 4 closed trades; TSLA was the biggest drag at -3,091 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 35 USD (0.03% of starting capital, 0.5 bp per fill, 0.6 bp of the 608,256 USD traded). 61% of that was spread/depth/impact and 39% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

9 closed round trips, 88.9% winners, profit factor 17.40, average winner +6,725 vs average loser -3,091 USD, median holding 197 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 251 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 1694 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: TSLA long 2025-09-17 to 2026-09-16, -3,091 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -45.05% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2026-03-27, recovered 2026-05-28. Average gross exposure 266% of starting capital, peak leverage 2.57x. Daily returns were +0.24 skewed with excess kurtosis +0.38; historical 95% VaR -5.51%/day with CVaR -6.71%/day. Worst single session -9.23%, best +12.35%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 35.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

Declared failure mode(s) that actually fired: Monthly rebalancing means the signal is stale for up to 20 sessions; 2x gross leverage doubles the drawdown in a trend reversal.

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.

Answer: did it work, and why

It worked: it beat the index by 36.80pp and the excess survives a beta adjustment (+4.37pp residual), so the outperformance is not simply leverage on a rising market. Finished at +51.21% against the S&P 500's +14.41% over the same 251 sessions - an excess return of +36.80pp.
