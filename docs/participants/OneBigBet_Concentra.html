Post-mortem: why it worked, or why it did not

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@OneBigBet_Concentra (One Big Bet) ran the "concentration" archetype. Declared thesis: Diversification dilutes return. Put the entire account, at maximum leverage, into the single name with the best risk-scaled 63-session momentum, and switch only when another name is materially better. Entry: Score every name by 63-session momentum divided by realised volatility; Hold only the top name at 2.0x gross; Switch only if the runner-up's score exceeds the incumbent's by more than 0.5. Exit: Switch, or the mandatory end-of-season liquidation. Sizing: 200% of equity in one name. Leverage: 2.0x gross, one position. Cadence: daily check, rare switches, horizon weeks to months. Aggression 5/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: The mathematically highest-variance legal book in the competition, which is what a highest-return-only mandate implies.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: +48.94% over 251 sessions - +48,936 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 148,936. Verdict: beat the market.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The beta premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+39.73% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path. The momentum premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+3.82% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the +48.94% total, about +7.99pp is explained by carrying beta 0.55 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.03); the residual +40.94pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. META contributed +45,362 USD (93% of net P&L) across 40 closed trades; CVNA was the biggest drag at -2,197 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 1,137 USD (1.14% of starting capital, 0.6 bp per fill, 0.8 bp of the 14,273,666 USD traded). 92% of that was spread/depth/impact and 8% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

55 closed round trips, 52.7% winners, profit factor 1.97, average winner +3,422 vs average loser -1,935 USD, median holding 1 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 251 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 670 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: CVNA long 2026-07-24 to 2026-07-27, -21,145 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -22.15% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2025-10-14, recovered 2025-12-29. Average gross exposure 74% of starting capital, peak leverage 2.15x. Daily returns were +1.25 skewed with excess kurtosis +14.57; historical 95% VaR -3.43%/day with CVaR -5.77%/day. Worst single session -13.12%, best +18.14%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 35.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.
