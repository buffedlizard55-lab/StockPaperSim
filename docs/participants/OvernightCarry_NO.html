Post-mortem: why it worked, or why it did not

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@OvernightCarry_NO (Overnight Carry) ran the "overnight premium" archetype. Declared thesis: A large share of the market's long-run return arrives overnight rather than during the session. This participant buys the index ETF at the close of every session and sells it at the open of the next, collecting the overnight leg only. Entry: Buy SPY at the close of every session, worked on the venue's final interval (at_close), so the fill is the closing print and the overnight gap is captured rather than paid away; Hold only the index ETF; no single-name risk. Exit: Sell at the next session's open. Sizing: 2.0x gross overnight, flat during the session. Leverage: 2.0x gross overnight. Cadence: daily, horizon overnight. Aggression 3/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Applies 2x leverage to the leg of the return distribution that the literature says is the paid one.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: +8.97% over 251 sessions - +8,974 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 108,974. Verdict: made money but lagged the index.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The beta premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+39.73% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the +8.97% total, about +0.57pp is explained by carrying beta 0.04 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.01); the residual +8.41pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. SPY contributed +7,767 USD (87% of net P&L) across 247 closed trades; SPY was the biggest drag at +7,767 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 1,156 USD (1.16% of starting capital, 0.2 bp per fill, 0.2 bp of the 52,070,789 USD traded). 58% of that was spread/depth/impact and 42% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

247 closed round trips, 53.8% winners, profit factor 1.25, average winner +290 vs average loser -270 USD, median holding 1 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 251 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 442 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: SPY long 2025-10-09 to 2025-10-10, -1,738 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -4.86% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2025-10-13, recovered 2025-12-12. Average gross exposure 103% of starting capital, peak leverage 2.03x. Daily returns were -0.01 skewed with excess kurtosis +2.74; historical 95% VaR -0.72%/day with CVaR -1.06%/day. Worst single session -1.81%, best +1.63%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 35.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

None of the failure modes declared in the strategy spec fired in this window.

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

Standing limitations of this simulation that apply to this participant regardless of the regime: TIMING / INTERVAL GRANULARITY: the entry is worked on the session's final interval, so it captures the overnight leg rather than the intraday one, but the venue's closing print is the last of K synthetic intervals rather than a real 16:00 tape print, and the exit pays the spread again at the next open. Both are cost drags a live implementation of this trade would also carry; neither is a reason to read the season's number as an estimate of the real premium. The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.

Answer: did it work, and why

It made money (+8.97%) but under-delivered versus simply holding the index (+14.41%); the signal did not pay for its own costs, drawdown and turnover. Finished at +8.97%, profitable but 5.44pp behind the S&P 500's +14.41%.
