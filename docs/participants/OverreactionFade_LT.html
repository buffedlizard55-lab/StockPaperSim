Post-mortem: why it worked, or why it did not

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@OverreactionFade_LT (Overreaction Fade) ran the "contrarian reversal" archetype. Declared thesis: Investors over-react to bad news. A large-cap that falls more than 5% in one session without a trend behind it tends to recover part of the move within days. Entry: Prior session return <= -5%; The 63-session trend must still be positive (avoids buying structural decliners); Up to 4 names, equal weight. Exit: Exit after 3 sessions; Exit if the position is up more than 8% (take the snap-back). Sizing: equal 40% of equity per name = up to 160% gross. Leverage: up to 1.6x gross. Cadence: daily, horizon 3 sessions. Aggression 4/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Buys the day everyone else is forced to sell and holds through the noise with leverage.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: +73.46% over 251 sessions - +73,459 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 173,459. Verdict: beat the market.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The reversal premium went against the declared exposure (-6.01% for a +1.0 loading) yet the account still returned +73.46% - the result was driven by something other than this factor. The momentum premium went against the declared exposure (+3.82% for a -1.0 loading) yet the account still returned +73.46% - the result was driven by something other than this factor.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the +73.46% total, about +7.03pp is explained by carrying beta 0.49 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.07); the residual +66.43pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. CVNA contributed +41,334 USD (56% of net P&L) across 29 closed trades; META was the biggest drag at +3,519 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 1,239 USD (1.24% of starting capital, 1.9 bp per fill, 2.0 bp of the 6,172,254 USD traded). 80% of that was spread/depth/impact and 20% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

70 closed round trips, 62.9% winners, profit factor 2.67, average winner +2,667 vs average loser -1,689 USD, median holding 4 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 231 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 1306 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: RIVN long 2026-03-12 to 2026-03-17, -5,343 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -12.47% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2025-11-18, recovered 2026-02-06. Average gross exposure 23% of starting capital, peak leverage 1.24x. Daily returns were +1.05 skewed with excess kurtosis +7.95; historical 95% VaR -1.60%/day with CVaR -2.89%/day. Worst single session -7.02%, best +7.90%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 35.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

None of the failure modes declared in the strategy spec fired in this window.

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.

Answer: did it work, and why

It worked: it beat the index by 59.04pp and the excess survives a beta adjustment (+66.43pp residual), so the outperformance is not simply leverage on a rising market. Finished at +73.46% against the S&P 500's +14.41% over the same 251 sessions - an excess return of +59.04pp.
