Post-mortem: why it worked, or why it did not

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@SectorRotator_AlphaX (Sector Rotator) ran the "sector rotation" archetype. Declared thesis: Capital rotates between sectors in multi-week waves. Buy the two sectors with the strongest 21-session relative return and hold until the ranking changes. Entry: Compute each sector's equal-weighted 21-session return from its constituents; Buy the top 2 sectors, split 100% of gross exposure between them; Use 2.0x gross leverage across the sector baskets. Exit: Rotate at the weekly re-rank; no defensive sleeve and no cash drag by design. Sizing: 1.0x of equity per selected sector, spread equally across its constituents. Leverage: 2.0x gross. Cadence: weekly, horizon weeks. Aggression 4/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Concentrates the whole account into the strongest two sleeves of the market instead of diversifying.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: +100.92% over 251 sessions - +100,921 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 200,921. Verdict: beat the market.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The momentum premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+3.82% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path. The beta premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+39.73% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the +100.92% total, about +35.13pp is explained by carrying beta 2.44 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.57); the residual +65.79pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. META contributed +39,594 USD (39% of net P&L) across 7 closed trades; GOOGL was the biggest drag at +11,237 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 48 USD (0.05% of starting capital, 0.3 bp per fill, 0.4 bp of the 1,089,270 USD traded). 60% of that was spread/depth/impact and 40% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

25 closed round trips, 100.0% winners, profit factor no losing trades, average winner +3,996 vs average loser +0 USD, median holding 100 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 251 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 3040 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: AAPL long 2025-09-17 to 2025-09-24, +62 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -32.15% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2026-03-26, recovered 2026-04-15. Average gross exposure 319% of starting capital, peak leverage 2.43x. Daily returns were -0.25 skewed with excess kurtosis +0.89; historical 95% VaR -3.77%/day with CVaR -5.71%/day. Worst single session -9.56%, best +8.15%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 35.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

None of the failure modes declared in the strategy spec fired in this window.

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.

Answer: did it work, and why

It worked: it beat the index by 86.51pp and the excess survives a beta adjustment (+65.79pp residual), so the outperformance is not simply leverage on a rising market. Finished at +100.92% against the S&P 500's +14.41% over the same 251 sessions - an excess return of +86.51pp.
