Post-mortem: why it worked, or why it did not

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@SpreadHarvester_MM (Spread Harvester) ran the "market making" archetype. Declared thesis: Provide liquidity instead of demanding it. Resting limit orders on both sides of the three most liquid names collect the quoted spread and the maker rebate, and pay for themselves as long as inventory does not run away. Entry: Each session, rest a buy limit one tick below the displayed bid and a sell limit one tick above the displayed offer; Trade only the three highest dollar-volume instruments; Size each quote to 10% of the session's expected volume, capped so gross inventory stays under 60% of equity per side. Exit: Quotes expire at the end of the session (TIF=DAY); If net inventory in a name exceeds the cap, cross the spread with a market order to get back inside it. Sizing: quote size from the liquidity cap, inventory cap 60% of equity per side. Leverage: typically < 1.0x gross; inventory is the exposure. Cadence: daily quoting, horizon intraday to a few days. Aggression 2/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Earns the spread and the rebate every session instead of paying them, and reinvests the accrual.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: -39.07% over 251 sessions - -39,073 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 60,927. Verdict: lost money.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The low_vol premium it is built to harvest went the wrong way (-21.86% over the season), so the signal was fighting the tape and the account lost 39.07%.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the -39.07% total, about -44.32pp is explained by carrying beta -3.07 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.41); the residual +5.24pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. QQQ contributed -29 USD across 4 closed trades; TSLA was the biggest drag at -16,317 USD.

Why: what execution cost

This participant was a NET LIQUIDITY PROVIDER: rebates (-163 USD) exceeded the fees it paid, so all-in execution cost was -76 USD (-0.08% of starting capital) on 22,555,469 USD traded. Gross friction was still real: 5 USD of spread, 4 USD of impact and 78 USD of fees - it simply earned 76 USD back for resting orders.

Why: trade-level behaviour

528 closed round trips, 70.6% winners, profit factor 0.71, average winner +247 vs average loser -839 USD, median holding 1 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 251 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 822 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: TSLA short 2026-04-08 to 2026-08-04, -38,605 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -57.39% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2026-09-04, never recovered. Average gross exposure 151% of starting capital, peak leverage 3.50x. 1 maintenance-margin breach(es) triggered forced liquidation: 2026-08-03: equity 57,080.46 < maintenance requirement 59,975.38 Daily returns were +0.32 skewed with excess kurtosis +2.59; historical 95% VaR -5.95%/day with CVaR -8.40%/day. Worst single session -16.11%, best +15.57%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 35.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

Declared failure mode(s) that actually fired: Adverse selection: the quotes fill precisely when the price is about to move against the book; Inventory builds in trending markets and the participant ends up long the losers.

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

Standing limitations of this simulation that apply to this participant regardless of the regime: DAILY DECISION GRANULARITY: a real market maker requotes in milliseconds; this participant requotes once per session, which understates both its earnings and its risk (flagged limitation) The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.

Answer: did it work, and why

It lost 39.07%, but the loss is a DIRECTIONAL one, not a trading one: after removing beta -3.07 against a +14.41% market the residual is +5.24pp, meaning the trades themselves added value while the position the account ended up carrying lost more. Finished at -39.07% - a 39,073 dollar loss on 100,000 of starting capital, in a market that returned +14.41%.
