Post-mortem: why it worked, or why it did not

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@SqueezeHunter_TF (Squeeze Hunter) ran the "liquidity squeeze" archetype. Declared thesis: When a name's daily turnover (volume / shares outstanding) spikes while price is rising, a large fraction of the float is changing hands at higher prices and short covering becomes self-reinforcing. Ride the squeeze for five sessions. Entry: Turnover today >= 2.5x its 63-session median; 3-session return > 5%; Prefer the highest turnover names, up to 3 positions. Exit: Exit after 5 sessions; Exit immediately if turnover collapses below its median. Sizing: equal 50% of equity per name = up to 150% gross. Leverage: up to 1.5x gross. Cadence: daily, horizon 5 sessions. Aggression 5/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Squeezes produce the largest single-day moves in the whole equity universe; concentration is the point.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: -1.85% over 251 sessions - -1,846 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 98,154. Verdict: lost money.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The momentum premium it is built to harvest was live (+3.82%) and the account STILL lost 1.85% - so the loss came from implementation, timing or leverage, not from a missing signal. The liquidity premium it is built to harvest was live (-10.84%) and the account STILL lost 1.85% - so the loss came from implementation, timing or leverage, not from a missing signal.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the -1.85% total, about +1.85pp is explained by carrying beta 0.13 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.02); the residual -3.70pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. TSLA contributed +7,014 USD (-380% of net P&L) across 4 closed trades; CVNA was the biggest drag at -8,452 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 479 USD (0.48% of starting capital, 1.7 bp per fill, 1.8 bp of the 2,634,338 USD traded). 78% of that was spread/depth/impact and 22% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

30 closed round trips, 53.3% winners, profit factor 0.92, average winner +1,291 vs average loser -1,607 USD, median holding 2 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 179 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 379 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: RIVN long 2026-08-03 to 2026-08-10, -4,741 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -10.76% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2026-04-23, never recovered. Average gross exposure 10% of starting capital, peak leverage 1.49x. Daily returns were +0.03 skewed with excess kurtosis +16.36; historical 95% VaR -0.86%/day with CVaR -1.96%/day. Worst single session -4.46%, best +4.49%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 35.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

None of the failure modes declared in the strategy spec fired in this window.

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

Standing limitations of this simulation that apply to this participant regardless of the regime: NO REAL SHORT-INTEREST DATA offline: turnover is a proxy for a squeeze, not a measurement of one (IR-07) The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.

Answer: did it work, and why

It did not work: -1.85% in a market that returned +14.41%, and a residual of -3.70pp after beta - the trading itself destroyed value, not just the market direction. Finished at -1.85% - a 1,846 dollar loss on 100,000 of starting capital, in a market that returned +14.41%.
