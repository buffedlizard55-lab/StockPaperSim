Post-mortem: why it worked, or why it did not

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@TrendSurfer_GoldenX (Trend Surfer) ran the "time-series trend" archetype. Declared thesis: Prices trend. A 20-session EMA above a 50-session EMA means the intermediate trend is up; stay long and add to winners (pyramid) until the trend breaks. Entry: Long when EMA20 > EMA50 on the prior session's closes; Add a second half position when price is also above the 50-session high; Maximum 5 concurrent names, chosen by strongest EMA20-EMA50 spread normalised by volatility. Exit: Exit when EMA20 crosses below EMA50; No trailing stop and no profit target. Sizing: 25% of equity initial, +25% on the pyramid = 50% per name. Leverage: up to 2.0x gross. Cadence: daily check, monthly-ish turnover, horizon weeks to months. Aggression 4/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Riding a full trend with leverage captures the fat right tail of price moves instead of clipping it.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: +30.11% over 251 sessions - +30,109 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 130,109. Verdict: beat the market.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The momentum premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+3.82% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path. The beta premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+39.73% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the +30.11% total, about +32.09pp is explained by carrying beta 2.23 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.76); the residual -1.98pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. NVDA contributed +10,476 USD (35% of net P&L) across 10 closed trades; JPM was the biggest drag at -524 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 41 USD (0.04% of starting capital, 0.3 bp per fill, 0.4 bp of the 1,008,728 USD traded). 61% of that was spread/depth/impact and 39% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

55 closed round trips, 89.1% winners, profit factor 32.83, average winner +601 vs average loser -149 USD, median holding 120 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 251 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 6920 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: JPM long 2025-09-19 to 2026-09-16, -461 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -26.86% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2026-03-27, recovered 2026-05-06. Average gross exposure 227% of starting capital, peak leverage 2.14x. Daily returns were -0.23 skewed with excess kurtosis +0.72; historical 95% VaR -3.17%/day with CVaR -4.61%/day. Worst single session -7.12%, best +6.48%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 35.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

None of the failure modes declared in the strategy spec fired in this window.

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.

Answer: did it work, and why

It beat the index by 15.69pp, but after beta the residual is -1.98pp: the win came from carrying more market risk (beta 2.23) in an up market, not from the signal itself. Finished at +30.11% against the S&P 500's +14.41% over the same 251 sessions - an excess return of +15.69pp.
