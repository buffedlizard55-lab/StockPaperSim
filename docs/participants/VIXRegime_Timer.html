Post-mortem: why it worked, or why it did not

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@VIXRegime_Timer (VIX Regime Timer) ran the "volatility regime timing" archetype. Declared thesis: This participant trades the one genuinely real exogenous series in Season 1: the daily CBOE VIX close from FRED. Equity returns cluster with volatility regimes, so hold the index ETF at maximum leverage when realised fear is high and the market is falling, and cut leverage when the regime is quiet and extended. Entry: VIX >= 24 and the index is below its 20-session mean: 2.0x long SPY (distress buying); VIX between 18 and 24: 1.5x long SPY; VIX < 18 and the index is above its 20-session mean: 1.0x long SPY; VIX < 15 and 21-session index return > 6%: 0.5x (extended, quiet). Exit: Regime change only; no stop loss. Sizing: leverage set by the regime table. Leverage: 0.5x - 2.0x gross. Cadence: daily, horizon all season. Aggression 3/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Concentrates the entire account into the index and varies leverage 4x between regimes.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: +28.36% over 251 sessions - +28,360 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 128,360. Verdict: beat the market.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The beta premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+39.73% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path. The low_vol premium went against the declared exposure (-21.86% for a +0.5 loading) yet the account still returned +28.36% - the result was driven by something other than this factor.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the +28.36% total, about +19.33pp is explained by carrying beta 1.34 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.93); the residual +9.03pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. SPY contributed +26,556 USD (94% of net P&L) across 35 closed trades; SPY was the biggest drag at +26,556 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 64 USD (0.06% of starting capital, 0.2 bp per fill, 0.2 bp of the 3,037,290 USD traded). 50% of that was spread/depth/impact and 50% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

35 closed round trips, 65.7% winners, profit factor 8.02, average winner +1,319 vs average loser -315 USD, median holding 132 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 251 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 4353 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: SPY long 2025-09-17 to 2026-04-06, -1,369 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -14.68% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2026-03-30, recovered 2026-04-15. Average gross exposure 140% of starting capital, peak leverage 2.05x. Daily returns were +0.27 skewed with excess kurtosis +3.21; historical 95% VaR -1.76%/day with CVaR -2.54%/day. Worst single session -3.51%, best +5.83%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 35.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

Declared failure mode(s) that actually fired: Regime thresholds (24 / 18 / 15) are hand-set and could be over-fitted to this window.

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.

Answer: did it work, and why

It worked: it beat the index by 13.94pp and the excess survives a beta adjustment (+9.03pp residual), so the outperformance is not simply leverage on a rising market. Finished at +28.36% against the S&P 500's +14.41% over the same 251 sessions - an excess return of +13.94pp.
