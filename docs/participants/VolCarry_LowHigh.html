Post-mortem: why it worked, or why it did not

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@VolCarry_LowHigh (Vol Carry) ran the "volatility risk premium" archetype. Declared thesis: Low-volatility stocks have historically out-returned high-volatility stocks because investors overpay for lottery characteristics. Go long the three lowest-volatility names and short the three highest, dollar-neutral, at 2x gross. Entry: Rank the universe by 63-session realised volatility; Long the bottom 3, short the top 3, equal dollar weights; Rebalance monthly. Exit: Monthly rebalance only. Sizing: 33% of equity per leg = 100% long + 100% short = 200% gross. Leverage: 2.0x gross, ~0 net. Cadence: monthly, horizon 1 month. Aggression 3/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Uses leverage on a market-neutral spread to convert a small annual edge into a large one.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: -29.99% over 251 sessions - -29,993 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 70,007. Verdict: lost money.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The low_vol premium it is built to harvest went the wrong way (-21.86% over the season), so the signal was fighting the tape and the account lost 29.99%. The beta premium it is built to harvest was live (+39.73%) and the account STILL lost 29.99% - so the loss came from implementation, timing or leverage, not from a missing signal.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the -29.99% total, about -18.83pp is explained by carrying beta -1.31 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.12); the residual -11.17pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. QQQ contributed +4,439 USD across 7 closed trades; CVNA was the biggest drag at -16,393 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 60 USD (0.06% of starting capital, 1.0 bp per fill, 1.3 bp of the 445,342 USD traded). 71% of that was spread/depth/impact and 29% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

36 closed round trips, 58.3% winners, profit factor 0.27, average winner +480 vs average loser -2,524 USD, median holding 104 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 251 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 4629 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: NVDA short 2025-09-17 to 2026-09-16, -10,777 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -48.17% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2026-04-17, never recovered. Average gross exposure 163% of starting capital, peak leverage 3.11x. Daily returns were +0.54 skewed with excess kurtosis +1.05; historical 95% VaR -4.56%/day with CVaR -5.58%/day. Worst single session -6.27%, best +12.91%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 35.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

Declared failure mode(s) that actually fired: In a melt-up the high-volatility short leg explodes upward; Borrow fees on the short leg accrue daily and are highest exactly on the high-vol names.

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.

Answer: did it work, and why

It did not work: -29.99% in a market that returned +14.41%, and a residual of -11.16pp after beta - the trading itself destroyed value, not just the market direction. Finished at -29.99% - a 29,993 dollar loss on 100,000 of starting capital, in a market that returned +14.41%.
