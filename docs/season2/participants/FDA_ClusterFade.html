Why it returned what it returned

Made money but lagged the index: +2.75% total return vs +14.41% for the index, +5.00pp residual after beta, -22.3% max drawdown, 0.45% of capital lost to execution costs.

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@FDA_ClusterFade (FDA Cluster Fade) ran the "contrarian reversal" archetype. Declared thesis: A burst of FDA approvals is a sentiment event for the whole biotech complex, most of which has nothing to do with the approvals. The fade participant shorts the sector into the burst and buys the drought. Entry: Compute the z-score of the trailing-30-day approval count against the previous 252 sessions (openFDA decision dates); Short XBI at 100% of equity when z > 1.5; Go long XBI at 150% of equity when z < -1.0. Exit: Exit when |z| < 0.5; No stop loss; the z-score closes the trade. Sizing: 100% short or 150% long of equity. Leverage: up to 1.5x gross. Cadence: daily check, horizon days to weeks. Aggression 5/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Two-sided exposure with no stop and a hard signal threshold.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: +2.75% over 251 sessions - +2,751 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 102,751. Verdict: made money but lagged the index.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The reversal premium went against the declared exposure (-4.85% for a +1.0 loading) yet the account still returned +2.75% - the result was driven by something other than this factor. The momentum premium went against the declared exposure (+22.09% for a -1.0 loading) yet the account still returned +2.75% - the result was driven by something other than this factor.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the +2.75% total, about -2.25pp is explained by carrying beta -0.16 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.01); the residual +5.00pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. XBI contributed +2,249 USD (82% of net P&L) across 34 closed trades; XBI was the biggest drag at +2,249 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 445 USD (0.45% of starting capital, 1.0 bp per fill, 1.3 bp of the 3,341,664 USD traded). 79% of that was spread/depth/impact and 21% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

34 closed round trips, 35.3% winners, profit factor 1.06, average winner +3,339 vs average loser -1,719 USD, median holding 10 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 222 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 720 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: XBI long 2026-05-26 to 2026-09-16, -12,547 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -22.30% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2026-09-15, never recovered. Average gross exposure 75% of starting capital, peak leverage 1.52x. Daily returns were +0.06 skewed with excess kurtosis +3.24; historical 95% VaR -2.72%/day with CVaR -3.53%/day. Worst single session -6.56%, best +5.32%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 103.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

Declared failure mode(s) that actually fired: Fading a sector in a genuine bull regime loses money quickly.

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.

Answer: did it work, and why

It made money (+2.75%) but under-delivered versus simply holding the index (+14.41%); the signal did not pay for its own costs, drawdown and turnover. Finished at +2.75%, profitable but 11.66pp behind the S&P 500's +14.41%.
