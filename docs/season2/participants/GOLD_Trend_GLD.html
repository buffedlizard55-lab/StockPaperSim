Why it returned what it returned

Mandate (as declared before the season)

@GOLD_Trend_GLD (Gold Trend (GLD)) ran the "time-series trend" archetype. Declared thesis: The GOLD project is a buyer's directory, not a market feed - its own README forbids price APIs. Rather than pretend otherwise, the gold participant trades GLD on the real ETF closes and uses the official LBMA fix only as a cross-reference. Entry: GLD close above its 50-session EMA and a positive 63-session return on the collected real bars; Hold GLD at 190% of equity. Exit: Exit when either condition fails; No stop loss. Sizing: 190% of equity in GLD. Leverage: 1.9x gross. Cadence: daily, horizon months. Aggression 4/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Full-size directional exposure to the metal.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: +32.83% over 251 sessions - +32,829 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 132,829. Verdict: beat the market.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The momentum premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+22.09% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path. The beta premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+8.32% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the +32.83% total, about +7.60pp is explained by carrying beta 0.53 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.02); the residual +25.23pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. GLD contributed +32,829 USD (100% of net P&L) across 43 closed trades; GLD was the biggest drag at +32,829 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 211 USD (0.21% of starting capital, 0.3 bp per fill, 0.7 bp of the 3,018,144 USD traded). 79% of that was spread/depth/impact and 21% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

43 closed round trips, 79.1% winners, profit factor 2.79, average winner +1,504 vs average loser -2,033 USD, median holding 87 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 234 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 3143 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: GLD long 2026-04-15 to 2026-08-31, -13,322 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -36.27% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2026-09-10, never recovered. Average gross exposure 150% of starting capital, peak leverage 1.99x. Daily returns were -1.61 skewed with excess kurtosis +11.25; historical 95% VaR -4.49%/day with CVaR -8.19%/day. Worst single session -19.31%, best +12.30%.

Regime context (real market data)

Season context: the real S&P 500 fell 9.10% from 2026-01-27 to 2026-03-30, VIX peaked at 31.05 on 2026-03-27, and cross-sectional dispersion was 103.8pp (stdev of individual 1-year returns).

Declared failure modes that fired

Standing caveats: what this simulation cannot represent

The style premia quoted above are measured on the simulated replay, whose cross-sectional drifts are DECLARED scenario parameters (see sim/universe.py). They describe this competition, not the real market: IR-16.

Answer: did it work, and why

It worked: it beat the index by 18.41pp and the excess survives a beta adjustment (+25.23pp residual), so the outperformance is not simply leverage on a rising market. Finished at +32.83% against the S&P 500's +14.41% over the same 251 sessions - an excess return of +18.41pp.
