Mandate (as declared before the season)

@MLB_Upset_Short (MLB Upset Short) ran the "event attention proxy" archetype. Declared thesis: A run of upsets is the scenario in which a sportsbook's hold compresses. The participant shorts the sportsbook complex after an upset-heavy week and covers when the rate normalises. Entry: Count upsets in the trailing 7 days (a win by the side with the worse win-loss record, from the official MLB schedule file); Short DKNG, FLUT and PENN at 50% of equity each when the count exceeds its trailing-60-session median by 25%. Exit: Cover when the count is at or below the median; No stop loss. Sizing: 150% gross short. Leverage: 1.5x gross (Reg SHO locate + Reg T margin). Cadence: weekly, horizon days to weeks. Aggression 5/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Shorts the same complex the long participant buys, so the season measures which side of the attention trade actually pays.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: -9.58% over 251 sessions - -9,579 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 90,421. Verdict: lost money.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the -9.58% total, about -0.29pp is explained by carrying beta -0.02 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.00); the residual -9.29pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. DKNG contributed -328 USD across 7 closed trades; PENN was the biggest drag at -7,539 USD.

Execution cost 586 USD (0.59% of starting capital, 4.7 bp per fill, 5.0 bp of the 1,173,887 USD traded). 76% of that was spread/depth/impact and 24% was explicit fees and rebates.
