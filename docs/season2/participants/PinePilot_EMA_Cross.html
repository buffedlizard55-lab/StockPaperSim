Mandate (as declared before the season)

@PinePilot_EMA_Cross (PinePilot EMA Cross) ran the "time-series trend" archetype. Declared thesis: PinePilot generates Pine Script, and the EMA-cross-with-ATR-stop rule is the canonical community strategy. It is reproduced here verbatim on real daily bars, so the result is the rule's, not a tuned variant's. Entry: EMA(20) above EMA(50) on the real closes -> long that ETF at 60% of equity (SPY, QQQ, IWM); Full position only; no pyramiding. Exit: Exit a name when its EMA(20) closes back below EMA(50); ATR(14) stop is declared but not implemented - flagged in the irregularity register because the community rule includes it. Sizing: 60% of equity per ETF, maximum three names = 1.8x gross. Leverage: 1.8x gross. Cadence: daily, horizon weeks to months. Aggression 3/5. This participant was built to maximise return, not to manage risk: Long-only trend following on the three most liquid ETFs.

Outcome (as measured after the season)

Outcome: +18.77% over 251 sessions - +18,766 USD on 100,000 of paper capital, ending at 118,766. Verdict: roughly matched the market.

Why: the style premia that were actually live

The momentum premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+22.09% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path. The beta premium this participant is built to harvest was live (+8.32% over the season) and the account made money - a tailwind that cannot be separated from skill on one path.

Why: attribution of the return

Of the +18.77% total, about +25.53pp is explained by carrying beta 1.77 against a +14.41% market (R^2 0.74); the residual -6.76pp is everything else - selection, timing, leverage and cost. QQQ contributed +6,416 USD (34% of net P&L) across 10 closed trades; SPY was the biggest drag at +5,047 USD.

Why: what execution cost

Execution cost 27 USD (0.03% of starting capital, 0.2 bp per fill, 0.3 bp of the 889,275 USD traded). 58% of that was spread/depth/impact and 42% was explicit fees and rebates.

Why: trade-level behaviour

49 closed round trips, 91.8% winners, profit factor 354.50, average winner +388 vs average loser -12 USD, median holding 112 sessions. Time in market, measured without double counting: 251 sessions held at least one position (the per-tranche holding periods above sum to 6116 sessions because tranches of the same average-cost lot share its entry date, so they overlap and must not be added together). Single worst trade: IWM long 2025-09-17 to 2025-11-14, -30 USD.

What the return-maximising mandate cost in drawdown

Worst peak-to-trough drawdown -12.10% (-9.10% for the index), trough on 2025-11-20, recovered 2025-12-10. Average gross exposure 184% of starting capital, peak leverage 1.85x. Daily returns were -0.25 skewed with excess kurtosis +1.09; historical 95% VaR -2.80%/day with CVaR -3.67%/day. Worst single session -6.52%, best +4.89%.

Answer: did it work, and why

It beat the index by 4.35pp, but after beta the residual is -6.76pp: the win came from carrying more market risk (beta 1.77) in an up market, not from the signal itself. Finished at +18.77% versus the S&P 500's +14.41% - effectively in line with simply holding the index.
